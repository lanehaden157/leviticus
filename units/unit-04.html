The Book of Leviticus · Study Translation

The sin offering

Unit 4 · Leviticus 4:1–5:13

    When the anointed priest sins · 4:1–12

    1 And Yahweh spoke to Moshe, saying:

    2 “Speak to the sons of Yisrael, saying: When a person sins unwittingly from any of Yahweh's commands that are not to be done, and does one of them—1

    The heading for everything that follows: wrong done without meaning to, against a command that forbids.
    Ezek 45:18–20. The prophet has the blood of a sin offering put on the doorposts and the altar's ledge on set days, "for everyone who strays and for the simple," to cover for the house. The rite for a person's error becomes the sanctuary's own cleansing.

    3 if the anointed priest sins so that guilt falls on the people, he shall bring near, for his sin that he has sinned, a young bull of the herd, whole, to Yahweh as a sin offering.23

    The first case is the highest rank: the priest's own wrong. So that guilt falls on the people keeps both readings open.
    Heb 5:1–3; 7:27; 9:7. The high priest is said to offer "for himself as for the people" and for the people's "ignorances." Reception, English only: the writer reads this rite as the pattern, and the Greek of these verses is not read here.

    At the entrance, then into the Tent · 4:4–5

    He brings the bull to the entrance of the Tent of Meeting before Yahweh, leans his hand on its head and slaughters it before Yahweh. The anointed priest takes some of the bull's blood and brings it into the Tent of Meeting.

    6 And the priest shall dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle some of the blood seven times before Yahweh, in front of the curtain of the holy place.

    The blood goes to the curtain that hides the inner room, the first step inside the Tent.
    Lev 16:14–19. On the Day of Coverings the same two acts, sprinkling seven times and putting blood on the horns of the altar, are done by the high priest for the Tent, "so he shall do for the Tent of Meeting." The same blood rite, on one day a year for all the people's wrongs.

    7 And the priest shall put some of the blood on the horns of the altar of fragrant incense before Yahweh, which is in the Tent of Meeting; and all the rest of the bull's blood he shall pour out at the base of the altar of the going up offering, which is at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting.4

    Two altars: blood on the horns of the inner one, the rest outside at the foot of the outer.
    Exod 29:10–14; Lev 8:14–17; 9:8–11. The bull of Aharon's ordination has nearly the same rite: hands leaned, blood on the horns with the finger, the rest poured at the base, the fat turned into smoke, the rest burned outside the camp; "it is a sin offering." There the blood goes only on the outer altar and Moshe acts; here the priest brings his own bull and carries the blood in. Exodus shows it once; Leviticus 8 and 9 narrate it done, and this chapter makes it the standing remedy.

    The fat, the flesh · 4:8–11

    All the fat of the bull of the sin offering he removes, the same pieces as are removed from the ox of the well-being offering, and the priest turns them into smoke on the altar of the going up offering. The bull's hide and all its flesh, with its head and legs, its innards and its dung, are set apart to be carried away.

    12 And he shall take out all the bull to outside the camp, to a clean place, to the ash dump, and burn it on wood with fire; at the ash dump it shall be burned.5

    The place outside is named clean: the burning is a rite, not a disposal.
    Lev 6:30; 10:16–20. The rule that a sin offering whose blood is brought into the Tent is burned, not eaten, is stated in 6:30. In 10:16–20 Moshe is angry that a sin offering goat was burned; Aharon answers that the blood had not been brought in. This verse is the case for the rule.
    Heb 13:11–13. "The bodies of those animals" whose blood is taken into the holy place are "burned outside the camp"; so Jesus suffered outside the gate. Reception, English only: the writer matches the exact scene here, and the Greek is not read in this unit.

    When the whole community errs · 4:13–21

    13 “And if all the community of Yisrael strays, and the matter is hidden from the eyes of the assembly, and they do one of all Yahweh's commands that are not to be done, and are guilty—6

    Second case, and the whole people: the wrong is hidden even from the assembly, so no one knew.
    Num 15:22–31. The same wrong, by the community, is dealt with there by a bull as a going up offering plus a he-goat as a sin offering; for one person, a year-old she-goat; deliberate sin is cut off. The animals differ from this chapter's; the two read as separate traditions of the same law.

    The bull for the assembly · 4:14–19

    When the sin is known, the assembly brings near a young bull as a sin offering and brings it before the Tent of Meeting. The elders of the community lean their hands on its head before Yahweh, and it is slaughtered. The anointed priest brings some of its blood into the Tent, sprinkles it seven times before the curtain, puts some on the horns of the altar in the Tent and pours the rest at the base of the altar of the going up offering. All its fat he removes and turns into smoke on the altar.

    20 And he shall do to the bull as he did to the bull of the sin offering, so he shall do to this one; and the priest shall cover for them, and it shall be forgiven them.7

    cover for them: the first of six times the priest covers and the wrong is forgiven; the anointed priest's own case has no such line.

    21 And he shall take out the bull to outside the camp and burn it as he burned the first bull: it is the sin offering of the assembly.

    When a chieftain sins · 4:22–26

    22 “When a chieftain sins, and does unwittingly one of all the commands of Yahweh his God that are not to be done, and is guilty—8

    Third case: the clause opens with ʾasher, not the earlier “if.”

    A he-goat, whole · 4:23–25

    When his sin is made known to him, he brings as his brought near offering a he-goat, a male, whole. He leans his hand on its head and slaughters it in the place where the going up offering is slaughtered before Yahweh: it is a sin offering. The priest takes some of its blood with his finger and puts it on the horns of the altar of the going up offering, and pours the rest at that altar's base.

    26 And all its fat he shall turn into smoke on the altar, like the fat of the well-being offering; and the priest shall cover for him from his sin, and it shall be forgiven him.

    From here the blood stays at the outer altar; the Tent is not entered.

    When one of the people sins · 4:27–35

    27 “And if a person from the people of the land sins unwittingly by doing one of Yahweh's commands that are not to be done, and is guilty—

    A she-goat, whole · 4:28–30

    When his sin is made known to him, he brings as his brought near offering a she-goat, a female, whole, for his sin. He leans his hand on the head of the sin offering and slaughters it at the place of the going up offering. The priest puts some of the blood on the horns of the altar of the going up offering and pours the rest at the base of the altar.

    31 And all its fat he shall remove, as the fat was removed from the well-being offering, and the priest shall turn it into smoke on the altar as an aroma of rest to Yahweh; and the priest shall cover for him, and it shall be forgiven him.9

    The aroma of rest, said of no other sin offering in the chapter.

    Or a ewe lamb · 4:32–35

    If he brings a lamb as his brought near offering for a sin offering, it is a female, whole. He leans his hand on its head and slaughters it at the place of the going up offering; the priest puts the blood on the horns of the altar and pours the rest at its base. All its fat he removes, as the lamb's fat is removed from the well-being offering, and the priest turns it into smoke on the altar, on Yahweh's fire offerings. The priest covers for him over his sin that he has sinned, and it shall be forgiven him.

    When wrong is hidden, and the scale by means · 5:1–13

    1 “And if a person sins, in that he hears the voice of an oath-curse, and he is a witness, whether he saw or knew, if he does not tell, he shall bear his iniquity.10

    The first of four cases where the wrong may be known to the person: a witness who keeps silent.
    Prov 29:24; Judg 17:2. “He hears the oath-curse and does not tell,” says the proverb of the partner of a thief; Micah's mother curses the stolen silver in his hearing, and he returns it. The same hearing of a public curse, kept hidden or confessed.

    Three hidden cases · 5:2–4

    Or a person who touches anything unclean, the carcass of an unclean wild animal, of unclean livestock or of an unclean swarming thing, and it is hidden from him: he is unclean, and guilty. Or he touches a human uncleanness, whatever the uncleanness with which someone becomes unclean, and it is hidden from him; when he knows, he is guilty. Or a person swears rashly with his lips to do harm or to do good, whatever a person may speak rashly in an oath, and it is hidden from him; when he knows, he is guilty of one of these.

    5 And it shall be, when he is guilty of one of these, that he shall confess that in which he has sinned,11

    Confession is spoken in the text only here in the chapter.
    Num 5:6–7; Lev 16:21. In Numbers the one who wrongs another confesses the sin and pays back the guilt; on the Day of Coverings Aharon confesses all Yisrael's iniquities over the live goat. Few places in the Torah ask for confession; this is where this chapter does.

    6 and he shall bring to Yahweh his penalty for his sin that he has sinned: a female from the flock, a ewe lamb or a she-goat, as a sin offering; and the priest shall cover for him from his sin.12

    his penalty, not “his guilt offering”: the sin offering is named in the same verse, as a second thing.

    7 And if his hand cannot reach enough for a sheep, he shall bring, as his penalty for that in which he has sinned, two turtledoves or two young pigeons to Yahweh, one as a sin offering and one as a going up offering.13

    The scale goes down by means, not by rank as in chapter 4.

    The two birds · 5:8–10

    He brings them to the priest, who brings near first the one for the sin offering and will pinch off its head from its neck without severing it. He sprinkles some of the blood of the sin offering on the wall of the altar, and the rest is drained out at the base of the altar: it is a sin offering. The second he makes a going up offering, by the rule; and the priest covers for him from his sin that he has sinned, and it shall be forgiven him.

    11 “And if his hand cannot reach two turtledoves or two young pigeons, he shall bring as his brought near offering, for that in which he has sinned, a tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a sin offering. He shall put no oil on it and shall not give frankincense on it, for it is a sin offering.14

    The lowest step: no animal, and the flour carries none of the gift offering's oil or frankincense.
    Num 5:15, 26. The jealousy offering has the same measure, a tenth of an ephah, and the same lack of oil and frankincense; a handful is burned as its reminder portion. Both are offerings of a person under suspicion or guilt, stripped of the usual gift.

    12 And he shall bring it to the priest, and the priest shall take a handful of it, its full grasp, as its reminder portion, and turn it into smoke on the altar, on Yahweh's fire offerings: it is a sin offering.

    The reminder portion is smoked on the fire; the rest is the priest's.

    13 And the priest shall cover for him over his sin that he has sinned from one of these, and it shall be forgiven him; and it shall be the priest's, like the gift offering.”

    The close of the speech; the next formula is at 5:14.

    Notes

    1. unwittingly (v2). shegagah is from shagah, to stray; the noun says an error, not a plan. What the person did not know is debated: that the act was forbidden, or that he did it. The heading says only “commands that are not to be done,” which is why it covers the cases of chapter 5 only in part. The verb in 4:13 is the same root. The Greek has “unwillingly.” Ezek 45:20 uses the root of the person who “strays.”
    2. so that guilt falls on the people (v3). le-ʾashmat ha-ʿam can be read two ways: his wrong brings guilt on the people, because they follow his rulings; or it is “for the people's guilt,” the people being implicated because he stands for them. The explanations given: he ruled wrongly and acted on it; the people stay under guilt because they depend on him; or he was less careful because of their guilt. The Greek reads “so as to cause the people to sin.” One view takes 4:3–21 as a single case, the priest's error drawing the community into error.
    3. sin offering (v3). ḥaṭṭaʾt comes from the verb “sin,” and “sin offering” keeps that link. But in this rite the blood is put on the altar and, for the priest and the people, the curtain, never on the person who sinned. So one reading calls it a “purification offering”: the wrong pollutes the sanctuary, and the blood cleans it. Another holds that every wrong stains the soul and the offering lets it approach again; another ties it to wrongs that would carry cutting off. The rendering here stays “sin offering”; the same noun is used where no sin is in view (12:6; chapter 14), and those notes point back here.
    4. the altar of fragrant incense (v7). Exod 30:1–10 puts it before the curtain; Exod 30:10 has Aharon cover on its horns once a year with the blood of the sin offering. Tentative: the Day of Coverings is the same blood on the same horns, once a year.
    5. a clean place (v12). The camp's outside has a clean place, so the bull is not carried to unclean ground. One reading holds the burned animal has taken in the strongest impurity, so it cannot be eaten; another that the burning is public, so the people see even the priest's wrong dealt with. The flesh of the chieftain's and the person's offerings is not said to be burned here; chapter 6:25–30 gives the rule.
    6. community and assembly (v13). ʿedah and qahal are used as synonyms, taken to alternate by editorial choice. Who acts is debated: the whole people, or the supreme court as their eyes. The elders lean for the people (v15); the people are not said to bring the animal themselves.
    7. cover and forgiven (v20). kipper here has a person as its object, which is why “cover for” is used; where the object is the sanctuary, a reading renders it “purge.” The line “and it shall be forgiven him” closes 4:20, 26, 31, 35; 5:10, 13. It is missing from the anointed priest's own case: one view says he must pray as well; another that the “for them” of v20 takes him in. The Greek has the same line.
    8. chieftain (v22). nasiʾ is the head of a clan or tribe, not a king. The clause opens with ʾasher, “when,” where the others open with “if.” One reading hears “happy,” on the rabbinic idea that a generation is fortunate whose chieftain brings an offering; another takes it as an inverted “if”; the plain sense is “when.” A he-goat is the usual sin offering of the people in later chapters (9:3; 16:5; 23:19); here the community has a bull and the goat goes to the chieftain.
    9. aroma of rest (v31). Said of none of the other sin offerings, and the fat is “as removed from the well-being offering.” One reading takes the phrase as carried over from that rite; the text does not say why it appears only here.
    10. hears the voice of an oath-curse (v1). qol ʾalah is a public call on witnesses to speak, under a curse if they stay silent. “Bear his iniquity” is the same phrase as for carrying the guilt of a wrong. One reading takes 5:1–13 as a separate offering, graduated by means and tied to wrongs that may be deliberate; the older reading took it as the poor person's version of 4:27–35. The rabbis called it the offering that goes up and down.
    11. confess (v5). hitwaddah, from the root of “give thanks.” The confession is asked here, where the wrong may have been deliberate; one reading holds that the confession belongs to every sin offering. Tentative: only a few places in the Torah ask for it.
    12. his penalty (vv6–7). ʾasham-wo is “his ʾasham.” The word elsewhere names the guilt offering (5:14–26), and the same noun is in le-ʾashmat at 4:3, “guilt.” But here the sin offering is named in the same verse, and a guilt offering would be a second offering. “Penalty” is the reading taken: what is brought for his sin. The Greek has a word for a fault or penalty at 5:6 and not at 5:7. “Ewe lamb” (kiśbah, 5:6) is a rare form of the word at 4:32. The guilt offering proper starts at 5:14.
    13. two birds (v7). “If his hand cannot reach” is the language of means. One bird is the sin offering and the other the going up offering, as in the cleansing of the Nazirite (Num 6:10–11), of a woman after childbirth (12:8) and of the one healed of flaking (14:21–22).
    14. flour as a sin offering (v11). It is the only sin offering without blood. One reading takes it as a concession for the poor; another points out that only blood covers the sanctuary, so the flour is an ambivalent step. Another says the sinner's offering must not be adorned, and a tenth of an ephah is a day's food.
