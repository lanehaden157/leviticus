# Unit 3 notes: Lev 3:1-17, the well-being offering (condensed)

English and Hebrew verse numbers agree throughout. Rulings used: well-being offering (shelamim), brought near, lean, dash, whole, fire offering, aroma of rest, going up offering.

## Scene and shape

People: the offerer (brings it, leans, has it slaughtered, removes the fat pieces, presents them); Aharon's sons, the priests (dash the blood, burn the fat; one priest at 3:11 and 3:16); no priest handles the animal's flesh in this chapter. Place: the entrance of the tent of meeting (3:2), "before the tent of meeting" (3:8, 3:13), "before Yahweh" (3:1, 3:7, 3:12). Objects: a head of cattle, a sheep or a goat, male or female, whole; the altar; the going up offering already burning (3:5).

Shape: a close variant of ch. 1 in three panels (herd, sheep, goat; each closes with a Masoretic petuhah after 3:5, 3:11, 3:17, per candidate-boundaries.md; Milgrom's own paragraphing starts a new paragraph at "All suet is the Lord's" in 3:16b). Same: bring near, whole animal, lean, slaughter, dash blood on the altar's sides, burn, aroma. Different: male or female allowed (1:3, 1:10 say male); no skinning or cutting up; only fat pieces are burned (3:3-4, 3:9-10, 3:14-15); the sheep adds the fat tail (3:9); no birds; the chapter ends with a lasting law on fat and blood (3:16b-17). Does: the chapter never says who eats. The meal belongs to 7:11-21 and 17:5; Milgrom infers from those and from narratives that the offerer's meat is the point.

Nearest same-shape passage: Lev 1:3-13 (unit 1). Second: Exod 29:13, 29:22, the same fat pieces from the ram of consecration.

## What the sources say

First, the text: male or female, herd, sheep or goat, whole; the offerer leans and presents; the priests dash; the offerer removes the fat pieces (the fat covering the entrails, the fat on the entrails, both kidneys with the fat on them, and the lobe of the liver, 3:3-4; for the sheep also the fat tail, 3:9); the priests burn them on the altar on the going up offering; all fat is Yahweh's and Israel eats no fat or blood, for all time, in all dwellings.

Milgrom: the name is an "educated guess" among about seven (peace, wholeness, communion, repayment, covenant, gift, the LXX's "salvific"); zebah is the genus (an offering whose meat the offerer eats) and shelamim the variety, three motivations (freewill, vow, thanksgiving), common note joy (Deut 27:7). Rejects Smith's mystic union and Rendtorff's two-sacrifice theory. Does not know why the fat is the Lord's ("shrouded in mystery") nor why the lobe. Fat tail: three views; he takes it as suet (as Ibn Ezra, Karaites, Qumran) against Akiba and Ramban as reported by him. The blood rite is "quintessential" (7:14, 2 Kgs 16:13); ch. 3 does not say the offering atones, and Maimonides is cited that no confession goes with it, though 17:11 treats it as ransoming life.

Rests on Milgrom's theory, not the text: that 3:17 and the words kol-heleb ("all suet", 3:16b) were added by H (argued from the closing formula at 3:16, which is complete once kol-heleb is removed, and the plural "your settlements"); that H predates D (so Lev 3:17 predates Deut 12 and dates the Priestly texts); that all table meat first had to be sacrificed.

Rabbis: Rashi on 3:17 only points to the Sifra; Ibn Ezra on 3:16: since fat and blood are God's, they are forbidden to you; Rashbam on 3:17: the ban holds even for animals not offered; Or HaChaim: the same, argued from "in all your dwellings", and notes Torat Kohanim. (Only these four short notes were in the extract.)

Morales: the order purification, going up, well-being = expiation, consecration, fellowship; the offering is the only one where worshippers eat with family and friends in Yahweh's presence; proposes zebah/mizbeaḥ as "meal"/"table"; cites others that the cult accesses life. Constable (popular, paraphrase): fellowship follows redemption; two proposals for the fat (the best, or something detested, citing Kiuchi); the blood rule goes back to Noah (Gen 9:4) and is explained at 17:10-12; likens it to the Lord's Supper. K&D, Ellicott, Pulpit, Henry: fat as "the best", fat tail, birds excluded for lack of fat (Henry; Milgrom rejects that reason).

LXX (Brenton English): "peace-offering"; in 3:11 and 3:16 "sweet savour" with no word for "food"; 3:2 "slay it before the Lord"; "habitations" in 3:17. The Greek not examined.
