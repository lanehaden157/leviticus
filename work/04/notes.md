# Unit 4 notes: Leviticus 4:1-5:13, purification for unwitting wrong (condensed tier)

Words: `kit.py words 4:1-5:13` (VERIFIED = Hebrew transliteration read there). Other books' Hebrew is read as pack leads (SHEET) or in English (ENGLISH). Greek of Lev 4-5 read with `lxx` (lemmas). NT Greek is English only. Verse numbers match the corpus throughout. Milgrom's word "purification offering" is his; the book's ruled term is "sin offering".

## The scene and its parts

People: Yahweh to Moshe, then Moshe to Israel; the anointed (high) priest; the whole community and its elders; a chieftain (nasiʾ); a person from the people of the land (Ibn Ezra: ordinary priests and Levites too); the offerer who "hears" an oath, touches something unclean, or swears rashly. Places: the entrance of the Tent of Meeting; the curtain (parokhet) of the holy place; the altar of fragrant incense inside the Tent; the altar of going up offering at the entrance, with its horns and its base; the place where the going up offering is slaughtered (north side, flock animals); outside the camp, a clean place, the ash dump. Objects: bull, he-goat, she-goat, ewe lamb, two doves or pigeons, a tenth of an ephah of fine flour; blood, finger, fat pieces.

Shape: one speech (4:1 to 5:13; next formula 5:14). A heading (4:2: "if a person sins unwittingly against any of Yahweh's commands that are not to be done"), then four cases, each opened by its own conditional: the anointed priest (4:3), the whole community (4:13), the chieftain (4:22, opened by ʾasher, not ʾim), the person from the people (4:27) with a lamb alternative (4:32). Then 5:1-13: four named cases (a witness who withholds, 5:1; touching an unclean carcass, 5:2; touching human uncleanness, 5:3; a rash oath, 5:4), one confession (5:5), and a three-step scale by means: female of the flock (5:6), two birds (5:7-10), fine flour (5:11-13).

Rite order for the bull (4:3-12): bring to the entrance, lean the hand, slaughter, take blood into the Tent, dip finger and sprinkle seven times toward the curtain, put blood on the horns of the incense altar, pour the rest at the base of the going up offering altar, remove the fat pieces (same as from the well-being offering), turn them into smoke, carry out the rest of the bull and burn it on wood at the ash dump. The community's bull (4:13-21) is abbreviated to "as with the first bull". The chieftain's and the individual's cases (4:22-35) drop the Tent: blood on the horns of the outer altar only, rest poured at its base, fat turned into smoke "like the fat of the well-being offering", and the flesh is not burned outside (the rule is stated at 6:25-30 and 10:16-20, not in ch. 4).

Masoretic breaks: after 4:12, 4:21, 4:26, 4:31, 4:35 (petuhah: five blocks, since the person's goat and lamb count separately), after 5:10 and 5:13 (setumah: the bird step and the flour step close the scale). 4:1-2 is the call and heading.

Seams: the closing formula "the priest covers for him and he is forgiven" (kipper + niphal of salaḥ) comes at 4:20, 4:26, 4:31, 4:35, 5:10, 5:13. It is absent from the anointed priest's own case (4:3-12); Ramban and Tur suggest why (the priest needs to pray as well); Milgrom, following Abravanel, says the plural "for them" at 4:20 takes him in. The pleasing aroma appears once, at 4:31 (the individual's goat).

Nearest earlier same-shape passage: the ordination bull, Exod 29:10-14, read in English (kit.py bible EXO): lean hands, slaughter at the entrance, blood on the altar horns by finger, the rest poured at the base, the fat pieces (covering fat, liver lobe, two kidneys) turned into smoke, the flesh, skin and dung burned outside the camp, "it is a sin offering". Same: nearly the whole rite, with the same rare nouns. Different: there the blood goes on the outer altar only, not into the Tent; Moshe acts; here the high priest brings his own bull and carries blood in. Does: Exod 29 shows the rite once, for the consecration of the altar; Lev 4 turns it into the standing remedy for wrongdoing. Lev 8:14-17 and 9:8-11 narrate it done (Moshe, then Aharon), both with the flesh burned outside.

## Differences between sources (say where they split)

- What the offering is: Milgrom, "purification offering" (the blood purges the sanctuary, never the offerer; the sin pollutes the altar, the shrine, at worst the inner room, in three stages); Rashi (from the Sifra) keeps "sin offering" for a sin of the kind that would carry cutting off; Ramban: every sin stains the soul, the offering lets it approach again; Morales' chapters read in this unit only say the expiatory offerings (purification and reparation) would in practice come before the ascension, tribute and peace offerings and do not treat ch. 4 verse by verse; Sforno: the priest's meat-eating is part of the atonement.
- "To the guilt of the people" (4:3): Ibn Ezra gives two readings (he taught wrongly and the people sinned following him, or "because of the people's guilt" and he did not warn them enough); Rashi: the Halakhic reading is that he ruled wrongly and acted on it, the plain sense is that the people stay under guilt because they depend on him; Sforno: the people's sinfulness influenced his words; Rashbam: the sages restrict it to a priest who acts on a wrong ruling, the plain sense is he is accountable for what his ruling causes; Milgrom: harm to the people, no certainty on the cause. Milgrom (after Abravanel) joins 4:3-21 as one case: the priest's error draws the community into error.
- Why the priest has no "and he is forgiven": Ramban (he must pray as well; his importance), Tur (a hint of criticism; a scholar's slips count as deliberate), Milgrom/Abravanel (the plural at 4:20 includes him).
- Who is the "community" (4:13): Rashi the Great Sanhedrin (as the Sifra), Sforno the court that should be "the eyes of the community", Milgrom: the whole people; `ʿedah` and `qahal` are synonyms, alternating perhaps by editorial updating.
- The chieftain's "when" (4:22): Rashi/Ramban from the Sifra, ʾasher is "happy is the generation whose prince brings an offering"; Ibn Ezra reads the clause as inverted ("if the sinner is the chieftain"); Ramban rejects Ibn Ezra, ʾasher = "when"; Milgrom: a stylistic flag, marking the shift to the lay type of sin offering.
- The burned bull outside: Milgrom (a "pure place", so not impure ground; the burned animal has absorbed strong impurity; priests cannot eat it); Tur: the burning is public, so the people see even the high priest confess; Rashi: Scripture says "clean" because Jerusalem has an unclean place outside.
- 5:1-13: Milgrom, a separate graduated sin offering (not just the poor person's version of 4:27-35), tied to prolonged impurity and the neglect of oaths; the scale is by wealth, not rank; the rabbis called it "going up and down" (oleh ve-yored); older scholars (de Vaux and others) took it as the poor person's option. Ibn Ezra: the cases are listed because the person must also confess. Ramban: the "oath of testimony" brings this offering even if wilfully sworn.
- Confession (5:5): Milgrom, required only where the sin may be deliberate; Ramban, in the plain sense the confession belongs in 5:5 because the oath cases are deliberate; the sages say all sin offerings carry confession (Tur).
- Fine flour for a sin offering: Milgrom, an ambivalent concession (flour can wipe away in Mesopotamian rites; but only blood purges the sanctuary); Rashi: no oil and frankincense because a sinner's offering must not be adorned; Ibn Ezra: a tenth of an ephah is a day's food.
