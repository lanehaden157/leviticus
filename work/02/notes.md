## The scene and its parts

People: the offerer ("a person", nepesh, either sex, Milgrom), Aharon's sons the priests (one priest does the handful), Yahweh. Places: the altar; the court. Objects: fine flour (solet), oil, frankincense, salt; oven, griddle, pan; unleavened cakes and wafers; first-ripe grain; the handful ('azkarah). Earlier appearances: the "aroma of rest", "fire offering" and "turn into smoke" refrains from 1:9, 13, 17 (pack: all three threads open in unit 1); "brought near offering" (qorban) from 1:2.

Shape: one voice, four panels, three opened by a conditional: (1) raw flour, 2:1-3 ("when", ki); (2) cooked, 2:4-7, three sub-cases (oven "when you offer", ki; griddle "if", 'im; pan "if", 'im), then a summary 2:8-10 that sends any cooked kind to the priest and the altar; (3) three prohibitions and a command, 2:11-13 (leaven and honey, first-processed gifts, salt); (4) the first-ripe grain, 2:14-16 ("if", 'im). Masoretic breaks: after 2:3, 2:4, 2:6, 2:13, 2:16 (so raw flour | baked | griddle with its crumbling | the three rules | first-ripe grain). No break after 2:7-12, so pan, summary and the leaven rule run on. The refrain "most holy ... of the fire offerings of Yahweh" closes 2:3 and 2:10; "aroma of rest" closes the handful in 2:2 and 2:9.

Nearest same-shape passage: Lev 1 itself, the going up offering. Same: a voluntary gift, three-step "choice of means" (there herd, flock, birds; here raw, cooked, first-ripe), the closing refrain of 1:9. Different: no blood, no slaughter, no wash; the offerer does little (pour oil, set frankincense, bring it); one priest does a handful; and most of the gift is not burned but eaten by the priests (2:3, 2:10). Does: after the whole-burnt gift for those who can afford an animal, a bloodless gift for anyone; Milgrom reads it as the poor person's going up offering (rabbis; Mesopotamian parallel). That placement reasoning is inference: ch. 2 itself never says "poor". Read: Lev 5:11-13 (the poor person's sin offering of flour, English), Lev 6:14-23 (priests' law of it, English).

## The commentators

- Milgrom: the whole chapter is built on four moves. (1) The gift is the poor person's going up offering, the rabbis' view (Midrash Leviticus Rabbah, Menahot, via Milgrom) matched by a Mesopotamian saying that the widow brings flour and the rich man a lamb. Inference from placement and the "if his means do not suffice" ladders of 5:7-13. (2) 'Azkarah is a token portion standing for the whole (pars pro toto): he lists four theories (memorial; the burnt part; the fragrant part; the "invocation" portion), rejects each on the evidence and says "no definitive answer can be given". (3) Frankincense is not required for the cooked kinds, as a concession to the poor (he reads the silence as deliberate); leaven is excluded as the symbol of fermentation, decay and death, not because the pagans used it (they used flour and oil too). (4) 2:3 and 2:10 (the priests' share) are a polemic: every non-priestly source burns the whole gift, and Jeremiah shows folk cults burning cakes to the "queen of the sky", so Priestly law gives the rest to the priests. That is a historical reconstruction and rests on his P/H and date schemes; the text itself says nothing about a polemic. Also: 2:14-16 is the barley omer, mandatory, reversing his own earlier view; 2:11 is addressed to priests (plural) while 2:13 is the offerer's duty (singular verbs).
- Rashi: the handful is what remains "remembered" for the offerer, "that part of it through which the owner is remembered for good" (paraphrase); the fist is measured by three fingers with the thumb and little finger wiping the flour (Menahot); "honey" is any fruit sweetness; the salt covenant dates to the six days of creation (the lower waters promised to rise as salt on the altar and water at Sukkot); 2:14 "if" means "when", the omer of barley.
- Ibn Ezra: 'azkarah, "memorial", is the owner remembered before God; others say it means scent, like Hos 14:8 in Hebrew numbering, Eng 14:7 (as Milgrom's third theory); the covenant of 2:13 is the oath not to offer something unsalted, because that insults God; 2:14 is a free gift, not the omer (no need for Rashi's reading); he reports, without choosing, that "some say" debash is date honey, with a proof from 2 Chr 31:5 (the translator's note gives the cite; Ibn Ezra's own text says "the Book of Ezra").
- Ramban: 2:11's "burn" verb is the incense verb; he cites Maimonides (the idolaters always leavened and sweetened their offerings and left out salt, via the Guide) but gives his own alternative: salt because food unsalted is disrespectful to a king (Mal 1:8); the plural verbs of 2:11 address Aharon and sons, the singular of 2:13 the offerer. He quotes Rashi on creation-salt as "a homiletic exposition of the Sages".
- Rashbam: 'azkarah belongs to the frankincense, whose fragrance rises and brings the offerer to mind (Isa 66:3, and the showbread's frankincense in 24:7); honey = tree honey, dates; 2:14 "when", the omer.
- Or HaChaim: the opening "and" (vav) of 2:4 shows the cooked gifts are read together with the previous paragraph, so their rules also apply to the high priest's gift; 2:14 "if" is timing only: the first-fruit gift is mandatory.
- Constable: the gift as tribute from vassal to overlord; Hebrew minhah also "tribute" (Gen 32:13); 'azkarah as memorial that prompts action on both sides; salt as covenant faithfulness; notes the grain gift "always followed" the daily burnt offering, which overstates (Lev 2 itself lists voluntary independent gifts; Milgrom's list of independent uses is in his Comment).
- Morales: the three voluntary gifts (going up, minhah or "tribute", peace) as an ideal worship order, with the minhah accompanying the going up offering (Lev 9:17 read in the translation, pdf pp. 145-146); his rendering "tribute" (the book keeps "gift offering").

Split worth saying: Milgrom against Ibn Ezra and the rabbis on 2:14 (obligatory omer vs free gift); Milgrom against Rashbam and Ibn Ezra on 'azkarah (token vs memorial or fragrance); Ramban against Maimonides on why salt and not leaven; Milgrom against the "Gentiles used it" reason for honey.
