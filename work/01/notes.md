# Unit 1 notes: Leviticus 1:1-17, the going up offering (full tier)

Words: `kit.py words 1:1-17` (VERIFIED = Hebrew transliteration read there). Other books' Hebrew is read as pack leads (SHEET) or in English (ENGLISH). The Greek of Lev 1 read with `lxx`.

## The scene and its parts

People: Yahweh, Moshe (outside, in the court), the Israelite offerer ("a person among you", ʾadam, men and women), Aharon's sons the priests. Places: the Tent of Meeting; its entrance (petaḥ ʾohel moʿed, the whole court in front of the Tent, Milgrom); the altar; the north side of the altar; the place of the ashes, east of the altar. Objects: a male without defect from the herd, flock (sheep or goats) or birds; blood; wood and fire; head, fat, innards, legs.

Shape: a call, then a speech, then a three-step ladder of means (herd, flock, birds). Each panel has the same rite order: bring near, lean the hand (herd), slaughter, blood dashed, cut up, wash, turn all into smoke. The flock panel abbreviates; the bird panel adds its own steps (wring off the head, drain the blood, throw the crop and feathers by the ashes, tear by the wings, do not divide). The closing line is the same three times (1:9, 1:13, 1:17): going up offering, fire offering, aroma of rest to Yahweh.

Masoretic breaks: 1:9, 1:13, 1:17 (so the three panels); 1:1-2 is the call and the speech.

Nearest earlier same-shape passage: the ordination ram in Exod 29:15-18 (lean hands, kill, dash blood around the altar, cut up, wash innards and legs, turn the whole into smoke, "pleasant aroma, a fire offering to Yahweh"; ENGLISH read; shared rare words in the pack leads). Same: nearly the whole rite. Different: there Moshe acts for the ordination; here the lay offerer does the first half and the priests the second. Does: Exod 29 shows the rite once, for the priests; Lev 1 gives it to every Israelite.

## Threads (pack)

All seven tracked threads open here and the pack's openers match: bring-near (1:2), going-up-offering (1:3), cover (1:4), lean-the-hand (1:4), aroma-of-rest (1:9), fire-offering (1:9), turn-into-smoke (1:9). Draft popover notes (copy ids from the pack):
- bring-near: yaqrib / qorban, the verb and the noun share a root. "Come near" is what an offering is. Reaches 142 uses.
- going-up-offering: ʿolah from ʿalah "go up". In this book it is the first offering taught (Lev 1) and recurs with the sin offering in Lev 9 and 16 (there the sin offering is named first).
- cover: kipper, first at 1:4, for the offerer. Milgrom reads "expiate/purge"; the book renders "cover". See translation-choices.
- lean-the-hand: the pressing of one hand on the head, ownership on Milgrom's reading. Contrast the live goat's two hands (16:21).
- aroma-of-rest: reaḥ niḥoaḥ, tied to Noah (Gen 8:21).
- fire-offering: ʾishsheh. Milgrom argues it is "food gift" (not from "fire"); the book keeps "fire offering".
- turn-into-smoke: hiqṭir, the altar's own verb; different from "burn" (saraf), which is for waste outside the camp.

Untracked roots that recur: tamim (22, reaches 1, 3, 4, 5, 8, 13...), zakar, ratson / ratsah (acceptance: 1:3-4, then 19:5, 22:19-29), samak, shaḥaṭ, zaraq, nittaḥ. Single notable renderings: murʾa / nosa in 1:16 (a "crop" or "tail region", unsettled, see the call), shissaʿ (split) in 1:17, malaq (wring off: found only here and 5:8, root unknown, Milgrom).

Translation-choices rows not tied by the pack but that apply: tamim "whole" (1:3, 1:10), minḥah, shelamim (mentioned in the intro only). The chat-flow Lane calls for 1:3-5 are in `translation-choices.md` (whole, accepted, lean, dash, cut up/pieces).

## Differences between sources (say where they split)

- Hand-leaning: transfer of sin (Shadal, Volz; Morales's critique), identification/vicarious substitution (Morales, "I am this animal"), ownership (Milgrom, from the verb's sense and rabbinic practice; the Hittite rite differs, it needs no leaning), declaration. Milgrom's decisive argument is the single hand (Ibn Ezra), against two hands on the live goat; Ramban says the rabbis read both hands (Sifra) and the singular only bars a proxy. Morales stresses substitution and says transfer would spoil the animal's blamelessness.
- "Cover for him" (1:4): Milgrom: this burnt offering's expiation is probably its oldest sense, later passed to the sin and guilt offerings. Rashi, from the Sifra: it covers neglected positive commands. Ibn Ezra: ransom. Ramban reads Ibn Ezra through a thought-speech-deed substitution scheme (the notes say it isn't verbatim in Ibn Ezra).
- ʾishsheh: Milgrom "food gift", denies fire; Rashi "of fire" (from ʾesh); the book keeps "fire offering".
- reaḥ niḥoaḥ: Milgrom "pleasing" (not "soothing"), because the sin offering lacks it; Morales "propitiating savor" (Geller) from the flood and 2 Sam 24; Rashi: it pleases Me that I commanded and My will was done; Ibn Ezra: quiets God's anger, or sends a power to rest on the person.
- Why these animals: Maimonides (via Ramban): to cure idolatry that worshipped herd and flock; Ramban rejects it. Ramban on doves: faithfulness.
- Moshe at the Tent: Milgrom, Sforno: Moshe waited outside until called; Ramban allows he did not know where the voice would come from; Ibn Ezra: he entered the Holy of Holies after the call (Milgrom says P bars that).
- 1:16: murʾa. Rashi: the crop with its entrails (Onkelos, Abba Yose ben Chanan), while the rabbis he quotes say the crop with the feathers on the skin; Ramban against Rashi, for feathers; Milgrom, "crissum" (the tail region around the vent) with "by its feathers" as a means. Unsettled.
- Hand-leaning and the LXX: LXX adds hand-leaning for the flock animal (Greek at the end of 1:10 in the lemma text read with `lxx`; the English of the Greek in `kit.py words` shows it at the start of 1:11), as the Hebrew has it only for the herd.
- Slaughterer: the offerer (Milgrom, Rashi: valid even by a layman).
