The Book of Leviticus · Study Translation

The well-being offering

Unit 3 · Leviticus 3:1–17

    From the herd · 3:1–5

    1 And if his brought near offering is a slaughter offering of well-being offerings: if from the herd he is bringing it near, whether male or female, whole he shall bring it near before Yahweh.1

    The speech turns at once to an animal whose meat is shared; the text does not say yet who eats. Male or female is allowed, where the going up offering of 1:3 asks for a male.
    Exod 24:5–11. The first well-being offerings in the Torah are of cattle, at the covenant on the mountain; afterwards the elders "saw God, and ate and drank." Tentative: the link is the offering and the meal before Yahweh, not the covenant rite itself, and it is argued whether the offering was needed for that rite.
    Lev 9:18–22. On the eighth day Aharon brings the people's well-being offering, an ox and a ram; the fat, the fat tail, the covering, the kidneys and the lobe of the liver go on the altar, and he lifts his hands over the people and blesses them. The rite of this chapter is performed for the first time.
    1 Cor 10:16–18. Paul argues from Israel eating its sacrifices that those who eat are partners in the altar. Reception, English only: Paul reads the eating of the offering as a bond with what is on the altar, which the chapter itself leaves unsaid.

    2 And he shall lean his hand on the head of his brought near offering and slaughter it at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting; and Aharon's sons, the priests, shall dash the blood on the altar all around.

    Three actors, as in chapter 1: the offerer leans and slaughters, the priests dash. No priest handles the animal's flesh here.

    3 And he shall bring near, from the slaughter offering of well-being offerings, a fire offering to Yahweh: the fat that covers the innards, and all the fat that is on the innards,2

    The offerer himself takes these pieces off the animal. Only the fat is brought near as the fire offering; nothing is skinned or cut up.
    Exod 29:13, 22. The ram of consecration at Aharon's ordination has the same pieces burned, in nearly the same words, "the fat that covers the innards"; there the right thigh is added. The ordinary offering of an Israelite shares the altar share of the priests' own.
    1 Sam 2:13–17. Eli's sons' servant demands raw meat before the fat is turned into smoke, though the man says "let them first burn the fat," and the text calls it a great sin, men despising Yahweh's offering. The story shows the rule broken: the fat comes first.
    Gen 4:4. Abel brings "of the firstborn of his flock and of their fat." Tentative, English only: the shared word is the fat, and the Hebrew there is not checked in this unit.

    4 and the two kidneys and the fat that is on them, which is on the sinews, and the lobe on the liver, with the kidneys he shall remove it.3

    remove is the same verb as "turn aside"; the offerer takes the pieces off the animal for the priests.

    5 And Aharon's sons shall turn it into smoke on the altar, on the going up offering that is on the wood that is on the fire: a fire offering, an aroma of rest to Yahweh.4

    The fat goes on a fire that is already burning a going up offering, which is why this offering comes second in the book.
    Lev 6:12. The priests' own rule says the same order: the going up offering is laid in order on the altar and the fat of the well-being offerings is turned into smoke on it. Verse 5 says "on the going up offering," and the later verse puts both on the altar: the order is the point, not where each lies.

    From the flock · 3:6–11

    Sheep: the same acts · 3:6–8

    If his brought near offering as a slaughter offering of well-being offerings to Yahweh is from the flock, male or female, whole he shall bring it near. If he is bringing near a lamb as his brought near offering, he shall bring it near before Yahweh, lean his hand on its head and slaughter it before the Tent of Meeting, and Aharon's sons dash its blood on the altar all around.

    9 And he shall bring near, from the slaughter offering of well-being offerings, a fire offering to Yahweh: its fat, the fat tail, whole, he shall remove close to the backbone; and the fat that covers the innards and all the fat that is on the innards,5

    The one addition to the list: the sheep's broad fat tail, taken whole at the backbone.
    Exod 29:22. The fat tail is among the pieces burned from the ram of consecration; the same word stands there beside the fat that covers the innards.

    The same pieces · 3:10

    The two kidneys and the fat on them, which is on the sinews, and the lobe on the liver, with the kidneys, he shall remove, as for the herd.

    11 And the priest shall turn it into smoke on the altar: the food of a fire offering to Yahweh.6

    Here a single priest acts; at 3:5 it was "Aharon's sons."

    From the goats, and the lasting law · 3:12–17

    Goat: the same again · 3:12–15

    If his brought near offering is a goat, he shall bring it near before Yahweh, lean his hand on its head and slaughter it before the Tent of Meeting, and Aharon's sons dash its blood on the altar all around. He shall bring near from it, as his brought near offering, a fire offering to Yahweh: the fat that covers the innards and all the fat on the innards, the two kidneys and the fat on them, which is on the sinews, and the lobe on the liver, with the kidneys, he shall remove.

    16 And the priest shall turn them into smoke on the altar: the food of a fire offering, for an aroma of rest. All fat is Yahweh's.7

    The refrain of chapter 1 closes the three panels; the last clause is a rule, not part of the rite.
    Lev 17:5–6. Animals killed in the open field must instead be brought to the entrance of the tent as well-being offerings; the priest dashes the blood and turns the fat into smoke for an aroma of rest. The chapter's rite becomes the only door through which meat is slaughtered.

    17 A lasting statute for your generations, in all your dwellings: y'all shall eat no fat and no blood.8

    y'all: the address turns from the single offerer to all Israel.
    Lev 7:23–27. The law is repeated with the animals named, ox, sheep and goat; whoever eats the fat of an offering by fire, or any blood, is cut off from his people. The fat of an animal that dies of itself may be put to other use but is not eaten.

    Notes

    1. well-being offering (v1). shelamim is plural and comes from the root of shalom, but nobody is sure what it names. Guesses include "peace," "wholeness," "communion," "repayment," "covenant" and "gift"; "well-being" is an educated choice, not a finding. The whole phrase zebaḥ shelamim, "slaughter offering of well-being offerings," says the animal's meat is eaten by the offerer; this chapter gives only the acts, and 7:11–36 gives the meal. The Greek reads "peace-offering."
    2. fat (v3). ḥeleb is the fat that can be peeled off the organs, as against the fat that runs through the meat; English "suet" says this but is an unfamiliar word for the ban of v17, so "fat" is kept. The same word is used elsewhere for the best of something (Num 18:12; Deut 32:14, as reported), but how the two senses are related is not settled. Tentative: Ezek 44:7 has "my bread, the fat and the blood," English only. The Greek has "sweet savour" in 3:11 and 3:16 and no word for "food."
    3. sinews and lobe (v4). kesalim is the place on the animal where the kidney fat lies. The older rendering is "loins"; it is reported that the word elsewhere names muscle or sinew, which is why "sinews" is used here, but this is not checked in this unit. yoteret is literally an extra or a remainder, and is taken to mean the lobe of the liver; the text does not say why this piece belongs to Yahweh. "With the kidneys" renders ʿal, "on" or "beside": the lobe goes with the kidneys.
    4. on the going up offering (v5). ʿal ha-ʿolah is kept literal. Readings differ: the fat is laid on the offering already burning; or the phrase means "with" the regular daily going up offering (Exod 29:38–39; Num 28:3–4); or "over," above the wood. Lev 6:12 settles only the order, going up offering first and the well-being fat after.
    5. fat tail (v9). ʾalyah is the broad tail of the fat-tailed sheep. Whether it is counted as part of the fat is debated: one reading takes it as fat, and another, mentioned in later sources, as a separate fatty piece. The text itself calls it "its fat" and burns it whole. "Close to the backbone" renders le-ʿummat he-ʿatseh, the side nearest the spine.
    6. food (vv11, 16). leḥem is "bread" or "food." Some scholars see here a fossil of the old idea that the offering feeds the god; none takes it so in Leviticus. Elsewhere the book uses "food" of the fire offerings (Num 28:2, 24), in the same words. The fire offering ruling stands; the Greek drops the word.
    7. all fat is Yahweh's (vv16–17). The closing formula is complete at "aroma of rest," and one reading takes "All fat is Yahweh's" and v17 as an addition to the chapter by a later hand, from the same circle as chapter 17. That rests on a view of the book's layers, not on the text. Why fat belongs to Yahweh is not said. The later readings differ on its reach: whether the ban covers only offered animals, or all meat.
    8. in all your dwellings (v17). moshbot, "seats, dwellings," is the word for homes anywhere in the land, not only at the sanctuary. Deut 12:15–16 allows eating meat in any of your gates, and says only that the blood is poured out like water; it states no fat rule. How "in all your dwellings" fits that is open: one reading says Leviticus is earlier and requires every meat animal to be brought; another reads the ban as reaching all meat, even of animals not offered. Tentative: Deut 32:14–15 uses the same word for the best of the flock and for Jeshurun grown fat; English only, no link is made here.
