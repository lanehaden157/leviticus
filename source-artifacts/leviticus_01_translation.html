The Book of Leviticus · Study Translation

The going up offering, the model

Unit 1 · Leviticus 1:1–17

    A call from the Tent · 1:1–2

    1 And he called to Moshe, and Yahweh spoke to him from the Tent of Meeting, saying,1

    The speaker is named only after the call. The voice comes from inside the Tent; Moshe is outside it.
    Exod 40:34–35; 24:15–18. At the end of Exodus the cloud covers the Tent, Yahweh's Presence fills it, and Moshe cannot go in. At Sinai he was called out of the cloud on the seventh day, then went into it and stayed forty days. Both have a call (qara) and a speech from a cloud-covered holy place; the difference is that at Sinai Moshe goes inside, and here he is called and stays outside, in the court. The book opens with law spoken from a place the mediator cannot enter, so every instruction after it comes from the edge: Sinai was a one-time meeting, the Tent a standing place where the voice is heard but the Presence is not entered.
    Exod 29:42–46. Yahweh says he will meet the sons of Yisrael and speak there, at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting, where the regular going up offering is made. The Tent is named for the meeting, and the going up offering is where the meeting happens.

    2 “Speak to the sons of Yisrael and say to them: When any person among y'all brings near a brought near offering to Yahweh, from the beasts, from the herd or from the flock, y'all shall bring near y'all's brought near offering.23

    any person, literally “a human”: the instruction is for all Yisrael, not for priests alone.

    From the herd · 1:3–9

    3 If his brought near offering is a going up offering from the herd, a whole male he shall bring near; to the entrance of the Tent of Meeting he shall bring it near, for his acceptance before Yahweh.45

    The entrance of the Tent is as far in as the offerer comes.
    Lev 22:17–25. The rule comes back for anyone who brings a going up offering: a whole male from cattle, sheep or goats, or it will not be accepted for him; the resident stranger is added.
    Lev 17:3–9. All slaughter must be brought to the entrance of the Tent of Meeting; the priest dashes the blood on the altar and turns the fat into smoke as an aroma of rest, and the going up offering is named at 17:8.
    Exod 29:38–42; Num 28:1–8. Two yearling lambs a day, one at morning and one at twilight, a regular going up offering at the entrance of the Tent; Numbers says it was instituted at Mount Sinai.
    Gen 22:2–13. Yahweh tells Avraham to offer Yitsḥaq as a going up offering; wood and fire go up the mountain, Yitsḥaq asks where the sheep is, and a ram caught by its horns is offered in place of the son. The same noun, the altar, the wood, the fire, an animal burned whole; but Genesis gives no procedure and puts a person where an animal should be, and it is not the first such offering (Noaḥ's comes before it). Leviticus gives the whole procedure to everyone: what was an act at a turning point becomes something anyone does on an ordinary day, with a bull, a sheep or goat, or a pair of birds. Later Shemuʾel, Manoaḥ and Eliyyahu each offer one at a turning point (1 Sam 7; Judg 13; 1 Kgs 18).

    4 And he shall lean his hand on the head of the going up offering, and it will be accepted for him, to cover for him.67

    No wrong is named. What the going up offering covers is left open.

    5 And he shall slaughter the son of the herd before Yahweh, and Aharon's sons, the priests, shall bring near the blood and dash the blood against the altar all around, which is at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting.8

    son of the herd: a young bull. The offerer slaughters; the blood belongs to the priests.
    Exod 24:5–8. Young men offer going up offerings and sacrifice bulls; Moshe puts half the blood in basins and dashes the other half against the altar, then dashes blood on the people and calls it the blood of the covenant. There Moshe does it himself, once, and some goes on the people; here the priests do it, routinely, and only on the altar. The covenant's blood rite becomes an ordinary act in any offerer's day.
    Exod 29:15–18. The ram wholly burned at Aharon's ordination goes through almost every step of 1:5–9 in nearly the same words: lean, slaughter, dash the blood, cut up, wash the entrails and legs, turn it all into smoke, an aroma of rest, a fire offering. Exodus gives it as a one-time act for one person; Leviticus makes it the template for anyone. These chapters, the first speech from the new sanctuary, come before the ordination story and explain it.

    6 And he shall skin the going up offering and cut it up into its pieces.9

    1 Kgs 18:30–38. Eliyyahu builds an altar, cuts up the bull with the same rare verb (also 18:23, 33), lays it on the wood, has water poured over it, and fire from Yahweh comes down and consumes the offering, the wood, the stones and the earth. Here the priests light the fire; at Carmel Yahweh's fire comes down on its own. The arranged offering waits for fire from Yahweh, as it will on the eighth day (Lev 9:24).

    7 And the sons of Aharon the priest shall put fire on the altar and arrange wood on the fire.

    Aharon the priest: here the title is his alone; his sons tend the fire.

    8 And Aharon's sons, the priests, shall arrange the pieces, the head and the fatty pieces, on the wood that is on the fire that is on the altar.10

    9 And its entrails and its legs he shall wash in water, and the priest shall turn all of it into smoke on the altar: a going up offering, a fire offering, an aroma of rest to Yahweh.1112

    Nothing is kept back for offerer or priest: all of it goes up.
    Gen 8:20–21. Noaḥ builds an altar and offers going up offerings; Yahweh smells the aroma of rest and resolves never again to doom the earth. niḥoaḥ comes from the root of rest in Noaḥ's own name. In Genesis it is a one-time response after the flood; here it is the standing result of every going up offering, tying each ordinary offering to the one that ended the flood.
    Num 15:1–10. A fire offering of an aroma of rest from the herd or the flock, going up offering or sacrifice, carried by the same refrain, and adding the grain and wine that Lev 1 leaves out.

    The going up offering from the herd · 1:3–9

    1. Brings a whole male from the herd to the entrance of the Tent of Meeting. 1:3
    2. Leans his hand on its head. 1:4
    3. Slaughters it before Yahweh. 1:5
    4. Bring the blood near and dash it against the altar all around. 1:5
    5. Skins it and cuts it up into its pieces. 1:6
    6. Put fire on the altar and arrange wood on it. 1:7
    7. Arrange the pieces, the head and the fatty pieces on the wood. 1:8
    8. Washes the entrails and the legs in water. 1:9
    9. Turns all of it into smoke on the altar. 1:9

    From the flock · 1:10–13

    10 And if his brought near offering is from the flock, from the sheep or from the goats, for a going up offering, a whole male he shall bring near.

    The flock repeats the herd's acts in the same words; the repetition is the structure.

    11 And he shall slaughter it on the side of the altar northward before Yahweh, and Aharon's sons, the priests, shall dash its blood against the altar all around.13

    The one new detail: the north side of the altar.

    12 And he shall cut it up into its pieces, with its head and its fatty pieces, and the priest shall arrange them on the wood that is on the fire that is on the altar.

    13 And the entrails and the legs he shall wash in water, and the priest shall bring near all of it and turn it into smoke on the altar: it is a going up offering, a fire offering, an aroma of rest to Yahweh.

    From the birds · 1:14–17

    14 And if his brought near offering to Yahweh is a going up offering from the birds, he shall bring near from the turtledoves or from the young doves his brought near offering.14

    young doves, literally “sons of the dove.” With a bird the priest does every act.
    Gen 15:9–10. Avraham brings a heifer, a she-goat, a ram, a turtledove and a young bird; the animals are cut in two, but the bird is not. The five are the animals of Lev 1, though the word for the young bird there differs, so the link is the turtledove and the unsplit bird. A covenant rite there, a daily rite here: the bird torn open without severing (1:17) is the one creature Avraham left whole.

    15 And the priest shall bring it near to the altar, and pinch off its head and turn it into smoke on the altar, and its blood shall be drained out against the wall of the altar.15

    16 And he shall remove its tail end with its feathers and throw it beside the altar, eastward, to the place of the ashes.16

    17 And he shall tear it open by its wings without severing it, and the priest shall turn it into smoke on the altar, on the wood that is on the fire: it is a going up offering, a fire offering, an aroma of rest to Yahweh.17

    The poorest offering ends with the same refrain as the bull.

    Notes

    1. And he called (v1). The first word, vayyiqra, “and he called,” is also the book's Hebrew name. Only here does the voice come out of the Tent to Moshe; elsewhere Moshe goes into the Tent and hears the voice from above the place of covering (Num 7:89). Meeting is moʿed, the root of the meeting times. The call is answered on the eighth day, when Moshe and Aharon go into the Tent, come out, and fire from before Yahweh consumes the going up offering (Lev 9:22–24). A possible link, thin because it rests on one verb and a mood: at the bush Yahweh calls Moshe and tells him not to come closer (Exod 3:4–5).
    2. any person among y'all (v2). The ritual texts of Yisrael's neighbours were often kept from ordinary people; this one is spoken to all the sons of Yisrael.
    3. bring near, brought near offering (v2). hiqriv, “bring near,” and qorban, the thing brought near, share one root (qarav). The same English verb serves for the offerer bringing the animal and for the priests bringing the blood and the parts to the altar (1:5, 13, 15).
    4. going up offering (v3). ʿolah, from ʿalah, “go up”: what rises whole in smoke. Another name, kalil, “complete offering,” stands beside it (Deut 33:10; Ps 51:19) and may be the older one. A possible support for the going up sense: the messenger rises in the altar's flame at Manoaḥ's offering (Judg 13:20); thin, since the Hebrew verb there was not checked.
    5. whole, acceptance (v3). tamim means whole or complete, without defect. ratson is acceptance: the offering is taken up on the offerer's behalf, not merely desired; the same verb at 1:4 says it will be accepted for him.
    6. lean his hand (v4). samakh is a pressing lean, kept apart from the ordinary verbs for “put.” Here one hand; on the goat sent away on the Day of Coverings, two (Lev 16:21). Whether the number changes the meaning is argued.
    7. to cover for him (v4). kipper, rendered cover; others give purge, expiate or make atonement. Unlike the sin offering, no wrong is named. The going up offering covers on the eighth day (Lev 9:7) and on the Day of Coverings, for himself and for the people (Lev 16:24), and with a sin offering for the Nazirite (Num 6:10–11). Possible further links, each thin: going up offerings for children in case they sinned (Job 1:5); Shemuʾel's lamb and cry before battle (1 Sam 7:9–10); David's offerings that end the plague (2 Sam 24:25). Lev 8:18–21 carries out these acts step for step for the ordination ram.
    8. dash the blood (v5). zaraq, to throw a liquid in a mass. The eighth day does it again (Lev 9:12–14). One reading takes the blood on the altar as life given back through it, which looks forward to Lev 17:10–14.
    9. he shall skin … cut it up (v6). “He” is the offerer, as in 1:5 and 1:9; the strip above places his slaughtering, skinning and washing in the court, which the text implies but does not say. The rare verb cut up returns darkly at Gibeah, where a woman's body is cut up limb by limb with no altar (Judg 19:29); a possible link, thin because it is one verb.
    10. fatty pieces (v8). peder, often rendered suet, the hard fat. The plain word for fat is kept for the well-being offering. The priests' side of the same offering is told later: it stays on the altar all night, and the fire is never to go out (Lev 6:9–13).
    11. legs (v9). keraʿ; some read the lower legs or shins. A possible link, thin because it is a shared list: the Passover lamb is roasted with head, legs and entrails (Exod 12:9).
    12. turn into smoke, fire offering, aroma of rest (v9). hiqtir is the altar's own verb, kept apart from saraf, “burn.” ʾishsheh, fire offering, may mean a food gift. reaḥ niḥoaḥ, aroma of rest: niḥoaḥ is from the root of rest, as in Noaḥ's name, and may carry the sense of appeasing.
    13. northward (v11). The flock's instruction adds only this to the herd's.
    14. turtledoves, young doves (v14). tor and bene yonah. The pair returns for the Nazirite (Num 6:10).
    15. pinch off … drained (v15). milaq, pinch off; the blood is not dashed but drained (nimtsah) against the altar's wall.
    16. its tail end with its feathers (v16). murʾah occurs only here. One reading takes it as the lower digestive end of the bird, removed with its feathers; another, ancient and widely held, takes it as the crop, and notsah as its contents. The ashes are told again from the priest's side: set beside the altar, then carried out of the camp to a clean place (Lev 6:10–11). A possible link: the altar's ashes poured out as a prophet's sign (1 Kgs 13:3, 5).
    17. without severing it (v17). The bird is opened but not divided, unlike the cut-up animals.
