The Book of Leviticus · Study Translation

The going up offering, the model

Unit 1 · Leviticus 1:1–17

    A call from the Tent · 1:1–2

    1 And he called to Moshe, and Yahweh spoke to him from the Tent of Meeting, saying,1

    The speaker is named only after the call. The voice comes out of the Tent, and Moshe is outside it.
    Exod 40:34–35. The book begins where Exodus ended: the cloud covers the Tent, Yahweh's glory fills it, and Moshe cannot go in. So the first word of Leviticus is a call to a man standing outside, and every law that follows is spoken from a house he may not enter.
    Exod 3:4; 19:3; 24:16. Yahweh has called Moshe before: from the bush, from the mountain, from the cloud on the mountain. Each time the call comes from the place where Yahweh is present at that stage of the story; now that place is the Tent. The difference is that at Sinai Moshe goes up into the cloud, and here he is called and stays at the edge.

    2 “Speak to the sons of Yisrael and say to them: A person, when he brings near from among y'all a brought near offering to Yahweh: from the animals, from the herd and from the flock, y'all shall bring near y'all's brought near offering.23

    a person is ʾadam, “a human”: the instruction is for all Yisrael, not for priests alone.

    From the herd · 1:3–9

    3 If his brought near offering is a going up offering from the herd, a whole male he shall bring near; to the entrance of the Tent of Meeting he shall bring it near, for his acceptance before Yahweh.45

    The entrance of the Tent is as far in as the offerer comes.
    Exod 29:42–43. The regular going up offering is made at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting, “where I will meet with you.” The offering is made at the place where Yahweh has promised to meet Yisrael.
    Mal 1:7–8. Centuries later a prophet scolds priests and people for bringing blind and lame animals, and asks whether a governor would be pleased with such a gift. The word for being pleased with the offerer is the word of acceptance here, and the demand for a whole animal is the demand of this verse. Tentative: the link is a shared word and a shared demand, not a quotation.

    4 And he shall lean his hand on the head of the going up offering, and it shall be accepted for him, to cover for him.67

    No wrong is named. What the going up offering covers is left open.
    Num 8:12. The Levites lean their hands on the heads of the bulls, one for a sin offering and one for a going up offering, “to cover for the Levites.” The same three words as here, lean, going up offering, cover, now for a whole tribe at its setting apart.
    Lev 16:21. On the Day of Coverings Aharon leans both hands on the head of the live goat and confesses Yisrael's wrongs over it. Here one hand, there two; here the animal is slaughtered, there it is sent away alive. Whether the number of hands matters is argued, and the contrast is a pointer, not a proof that one rite carries wrongs and the other does not.

    5 And he shall slaughter the son of the herd before Yahweh; and Aharon's sons, the priests, shall bring near the blood and dash the blood against the altar all around, which is at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting.8

    son of the herd: a young bull. The offerer slaughters; the blood belongs to the priests.
    Exod 29:15–18. The ordination ram goes through nearly all of 1:5–9 in nearly the same words: hands leaned, slaughter, blood dashed around the altar, cut into pieces, entrails and legs washed, all turned into smoke, a fire offering, an aroma of rest. There Moshe does the rite once, for Aharon and his sons; here the offerer does the first half and the priests the second, and it is given to every Israelite.
    Exod 24:5–8. At the covenant on Sinai there are going up offerings, and Moshe dashes half the blood against the altar and the rest on the people. Tentative: the shared verb and the blood at the altar tie the two, but there the people are touched by the blood and here only the altar is.

    6 And he shall skin the going up offering and cut it up into its pieces.9

    7 And the sons of Aharon the priest shall put fire on the altar and arrange wood on the fire.

    Aharon the priest: here the title is his alone; his sons tend the fire.
    Gen 22:3–9. Avraham splits wood for a going up offering, arranges it on the altar, and lays his son on the wood. The shared words are the going up offering and the arranging of wood, and little else. There is no procedure and the victim is a person; Yahweh supplies a ram. Tentative: that wood on an altar can recall Yitsḥaq is a reader's association, not a link the text makes.

    8 And Aharon's sons, the priests, shall arrange the pieces, the head and the fatty pieces, on the wood that is on the fire that is on the altar.

    9 And its entrails and its legs he shall wash in water; and the priest shall turn all of it into smoke on the altar: a going up offering, a fire offering, an aroma of rest to Yahweh.101112

    Nothing is kept back for offerer or priest: all of it goes up.
    Gen 8:20–21. After the flood Noaḥ builds an altar and offers going up offerings, and Yahweh smells the aroma of rest and resolves never again to curse the ground. In Genesis it is a single answer at a turning point; here it is the refrain of every going up offering, so each ordinary offering is tied to the one that ended the flood.
    Num 28:2–3. The daily going up offering, two lambs a year old without defect, is “a fire offering, an aroma of rest” in the same words. What Leviticus teaches any person to do, Numbers fixes as the people's daily routine.
    Lev 9:22–24. On the eighth day, after Aharon has made his offerings, fire comes from before Yahweh and consumes the going up offering on the altar. Tentative: the first rite taught here is the one the glory answers when the Tent opens for use. The link is the same event, not shared wording.
    Ps 40:6–8 and Heb 10:5–10. The psalmist says Yahweh did not delight in sacrifice or in the going up offering and answers, “I have come to do your will.” Hebrews takes the psalm and reads it of Christ, whose body, it says, replaces the offerings once for all. This is reception, not what Leviticus says of the rite.

    The going up offering from the herd · 1:3–9

    1. Brings a whole male from the herd to the entrance of the Tent of Meeting. 1:3
    2. Leans his hand on its head. 1:4
    3. Slaughters it before Yahweh. 1:5
    4. The priests bring the blood near and dash it against the altar all around. 1:5
    5. Skins it and cuts it up into its pieces. 1:6
    6. The priests put fire on the altar and arrange wood on the fire. 1:7
    7. The priests arrange the pieces, the head and the fatty pieces on the wood. 1:8
    8. Washes the entrails and the legs in water. 1:9
    9. The priest turns all of it into smoke on the altar. 1:9

    From the flock · 1:10–13

    10 And if his brought near offering is from the flock, from the young sheep or from the goats, for a going up offering, a whole male he shall bring it near.

    The flock repeats the herd's acts in the same words; the repetition is the structure.

    11 And he shall slaughter it on the flank of the altar, northward, before Yahweh; and Aharon's sons, the priests, shall dash its blood against the altar all around.13

    The one new detail: the north side of the altar.

    12 And he shall cut it up into its pieces, with its head and its fatty pieces, and the priest shall arrange them on the wood that is on the fire that is on the altar.

    13 And the entrails and the legs he shall wash in water; and the priest shall bring all of it near and turn it into smoke on the altar: it is a going up offering, a fire offering, an aroma of rest to Yahweh.

    From the birds · 1:14–17

    14 And if his brought near offering to Yahweh is a going up offering from the birds, he shall bring near from the turtledoves or from the young doves his brought near offering.14

    young doves, literally “sons of the dove.” With a bird the priest does every act.

    15 And the priest shall bring it near to the altar, and pinch off its head and turn it into smoke on the altar; and its blood shall be drained out against the wall of the altar.15

    16 And he shall remove its crop with its feathers and throw it beside the altar, eastward, to the place of the ashes.16

    17 And he shall split it open by its wings; he shall not sever it. And the priest shall turn it into smoke on the altar, on the wood that is on the fire: it is a going up offering, a fire offering, an aroma of rest to Yahweh.17

    The poorest offering ends with the same refrain as the bull.
    Gen 15:9–10. For the covenant Avraham brings a heifer, a she-goat, a ram, a turtledove and a young bird; he cuts the animals in two but not the birds. The five creatures are the five of this chapter, and the birds are left whole in both places, though the word for the young bird differs. There it is a covenant night, here a rite any day; the one detail kept is the bird not divided.

    Notes

    1. And he called (v1). The first word, vayyiqra, “and he called,” is the book's Hebrew name, and the “and” links it to the end of Exodus. Only here does the voice come out of the Tent to Moshe. Readers differ on whether he waited outside until called, or did not yet know where the voice would come from; one old reading has him enter the innermost room after the call, which the priestly texts do not otherwise allow. The call is answered on the eighth day, when Moshe and Aharon go into the Tent and come out (Lev 9:23).
    2. a person, from among y'all (v2). ʾadam includes women as well as men, and here only Israelites. The Hebrew puts “from y'all” after the verb, and the phrase can be read with the person (“any of you”) or with the offering (“an offering from you”). The instruction is addressed to the whole people, not to priests alone; a ritual open to every reader is not what neighbouring cultures' temple texts usually give.
    3. bring near, brought near offering (v2). hiqriv, “bring near,” and qorban, the thing brought near, share the root qarav. The same English verb serves for the offerer bringing the animal and for the priests bringing the blood and the parts to the altar (1:5, 13, 15). Animals is behemah, domestic beasts, not wild game.
    4. going up offering (v3). ʿolah, from ʿalah, “go up”: what rises in smoke. Another name, kalil, “complete offering,” stands beside it in older poetry (Deut 33:10; Ps 51:19) and may be the older word. The entrance of the Tent of Meeting is probably the whole court in front of the Tent, not a doorway.
    5. whole, acceptance (v3). tamim means complete, without defect; the same word runs through the later rule for the going up offering (Lev 22:19–21), where an animal with a defect “will not be accepted.” ratson is acceptance, from the verb that says in v4 the offering “shall be accepted.” for his acceptance can also be read “to be accepted for him” or “for his favour.” The Hebrew leaves open whether the animal or the offerer is being accepted.
    6. lean his hand (v4). samakh is a pressing lean, not a light touch. What it does is argued. One reading has the offerer hand his wrongs to the animal; another, that he identifies himself with it (“this animal is me”); another, that he marks it as his own. The strongest textual point for the last is the single hand here against the two on the goat sent away (Lev 16:21), where wrongs are confessed; the answer is that later rabbinic readers took two hands for every offering and the singular as barring a proxy. The verse itself says only that leaning is how it comes to be accepted for him.
    7. to cover for him (v4). kipper, rendered cover; others give purge, expiate, make atonement, ransom. A voluntary gift is said at its first mention to do something about guilt, though no wrong is named. One reading takes this as the offering's oldest function, which the sin and guilt offerings of the next chapters later took over; another, from the rabbis, says it covers neglected commands; another, ransom. The going up offering covers on the eighth day (Lev 9:7), on the Day of Coverings (Lev 16:24), and with a sin offering for the Nazirite (Num 6:10–11).
    8. dash the blood (v5). zaraq, to throw a liquid in a mass, not to sprinkle. The blood is not burned; one reading takes it as the animal's life given back to Yahweh. The same act is done again on the eighth day (Lev 9:12–14), and the reading prepares Lev 17:10–14.
    9. he shall skin … cut it up (v6). “He” is the offerer, as in 1:5 and 1:9; the strip above places his slaughtering, skinning and washing in the court, which the text implies but does not say. The rare verb cut up (nittaḥ) recurs for the ordination ram (Exod 29:17) and in the account of a woman's body cut up limb by limb (Judg 19:29); the second is a shared verb, not a shared scene.
    10. fatty pieces (v8). peder, the hard fat of an animal, often rendered suet. The ordinary word for fat is kept for the well-being offering. The priests' side of the same offering is told later: it stays on the altar all night, and the fire is never to go out (Lev 6:9–13).
    11. legs (v9). keraʿ; some read the lower legs. Entrails is qereb, the inner parts; both are washed before they go to the altar, the one place in the rite where the animal is washed.
    12. turn into smoke, fire offering, aroma of rest (v9). hiqtir is the altar's own verb, kept apart from saraf, “burn,” used for what is burned away outside the camp. ʾishsheh, fire offering: some derive it from “fire” and others read it “food gift,” since it is also used of bread, which is not burned. reaḥ niḥoaḥ, aroma of rest: niḥoaḥ is from the root of rest, as in Noaḥ's name. Readers differ whether the sense is “soothing” (turning away anger), “pleasing,” or Yahweh's pleasure that his command was done.
    13. northward (v11). The flock's instruction adds only this to the herd's: the animal is slaughtered at the north flank of the altar, perhaps because the other sides were taken by the ramp, the basin and the ashes. Why the herd has no side named is not said.
    14. turtledoves, young doves (v14). tor and bene yonah, literally “sons of the dove.” Unlike the herd and flock, the text says nothing of a male or of a whole animal for the birds, perhaps because a bird's sex and defects are hard to check, perhaps to open the offering to those with little. The pair returns for the Nazirite (Num 6:10).
    15. pinch off (v15). malaq, found only here and in the sin offering of birds (5:8). Pinch off follows the later tradition that the priest nips the neck with his nail; “wring off” and “twist off” are the other common renderings. The blood is not dashed but drained (nimtsah) against the altar's wall. The bird's offerer does not lean a hand: the priest holds the bird.
    16. its crop with its feathers (v16). murʾah occurs only here. The rendering follows one old reading: the crop, with the feathers on its skin, taken out and thrown on the ash heap. Others read “crop with its contents” or the tail end around the vent, removed with its feathers; one such reading takes “by its feathers” as a means, “by the tail feathers.” The rendering is a choice among these. The ashes are told again from the priest's side: set beside the altar, then carried out of the camp to a clean place (Lev 6:10–11).
    17. split it open, not sever it (v17). shissaʿ, split or tear open; loʾ yabdil, “he shall not divide.” The bird is opened by its wings but kept whole, unlike the cut-up animals. One reading takes the wings left on as making the small gift look larger.
