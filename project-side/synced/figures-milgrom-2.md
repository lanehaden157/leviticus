## B. Structures set as text (no placeholder)

### B8. Chapter 17 (pp. 1448-49, text): five laws, one centre
Concerns: 17:1-16. Milgrom (after Schwartz): five laws arranged as an inverted V; the central law (vv. 10-12, no blood) is the axis, and carries the unique "I will set my face / cut off" emphasis.
Facts: (1) vv. 3-7 slaughter only at a legitimate sanctuary; (2) 8-9 no sacrifice to other gods (Israelite and ger); (3) **10-12 no eating blood (axis)**; (4) 13-14 blood of game must be buried; (5) 15-16 eating a carcass. Penalties ascend to law 3 then descend. Also: 17:10-12 is itself AXA' with the ransom reason at the centre (blood on the altar ransoms the eater from murder-guilt); 17:13-14 is ABCC'B'A'.
Feeds: **unit map** (ch. 17 as the bridge between P and H), **ritual** (slaughter and blood), **tabernacle map layer** (everything slaughtered must reach the altar). Text only.

### B9. Chapter 18 (pp. 1516-24, text): AXA' frame, and the full list of 6-23
Concerns: 18:1-30.
Facts: **Frame**: opening exhortation 2b-5 (YHWH's self-introduction, ban on Egypt's and Canaan's ways, keep my laws); the list X = 6-23; closing exhortation 24-30 (the nations defiled the land, the land vomits them out, threat of expulsion and karet, "I am YHWH"). "I am YHWH" appears seven times (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 21, 30). The opening echoes the Sinai self-declaration; Milgrom reads this as making the sex laws "equivalent in importance to the Decalogue." No penalty is stated except karet in 29.
**The list (Milgrom's outline, pp. 1523-24):** I. incest: A. primary: 6 general; 7 mother; 8 father's wife; 9 half-sister; 10 granddaughter; 11 stepsister; 12 father's sister; 13 mother's sister; 14 father's brother's wife; 15 daughter-in-law; 16 brother's wife; 17a woman and her daughter. B. extra: 17b woman and her granddaughter; 18 wife's sister (rival wife). II. other: 19 menstruant; 20 neighbor's wife (adultery); 21 Molek; 22 male homosexuality; 23 bestiality, male and female.
Other groupings: Paton: two pentads (6-10 first-degree, 11-15 second-degree), then 16-19 relatives by marriage and 20-23 outside the family. Masoretic spacing: 6-17 separated by closed divisions, 18-23 run on (no "and" links in the first group because all are kin). Rationales: social (household peace) for 6-18, 20; procreation for 19, 21-23 (Ramban).
Feeds: **lawparallel** (the Lev 18 column), **unit map**, **condensed table**. Text only; B11 for the concordance.

### B10. Chapter 19 (pp. 1596-1601, text): the unit list and the structure options
Concerns: 19:1-37, the central chapter of the book (and, Milgrom argues, of the Torah).
Facts: heading 2 ("You shall be holy"). **Milgrom's own counts**: usual division, 14 units in three sections (B religious 2b-10: be holy; parents and sabbath; no idolatry; sacrificial and ordinary food; C ethical 11-18: honesty, no exploitation, justice, love of the fellow; D miscellaneous 19-37: mixed breeding 19-25; pagan practices 26-28; prostitution and sanctuary 29-30; necromancy 31; elders 32; the ger 33-34; honest trading 36; closing 37). Schwartz: 18 units plus heading and closing (units 1-9 end at 19; 10-18 end at 37).
The formulas: "I YHWH your God" closes units 1-4, 9, 12, 14, 15; the short "I YHWH" closes 5-8, 10, 11, 13, 16. Counts: long formula 1+7 times (vv. 2, 3, 4, 10, 25, 31, 34, 36); short formula 7+1 times. Religious duties use the long formula, ethical duties the short.
**Structure options**: (a) three sections as above; (b) Magonet: three parts 1-18, 19-29, 30-37, each headed by "keep"; first and third share six phrases (v. 3b/30a, 4a/31, 14c/32c, 15a/35a, 18c/34b, 4/37a); Milgrom's reading is **AXA'**: panels 1-18 and 30-37 locked by chiasm, centre X = 19-29; (c) Crüsemann/Ruwe: two halves matching theme for theme (A3), rejected as forced; (d) Schwartz's two-stage growth: original = heading + 16 units (2-32) + closing 37; 33-36 added.
Ritual vs ethical (p. 1598): ethical units 1*, 4-8, 13, 16-18; ritual units 1*-3, 9-12, 14-15 (Milgrom's count). H's "revolution": holiness once limited to priests and sanctuary is now reachable by every Israelite who keeps the cultic bans and the ethical duties. Schwartz's objection (8 of 18 units lack the root qds) is answered in the COMMENT (holiness breakdown, pp. 1722-24, where the unit-by-unit "how it is holiness" explanation is given: units 1-2 echo the Decalogue; 3, 11 and 9 are about the sacred sphere; 4, 6-8 about compassion as God's attribute; 12 about death; 13 prostitution as desecration; 14-16 echo 3-4).
Feeds: **unit map / peaks** (19 is a peak unit), **lawparallel** (Lev 19 against Exod 20/Deut 5; units 4, 13 against Deut 24), **qadash**. Text only; uncertainty: Milgrom switches unit counts (14, 16, 18) between the intro and the commentary; use the unit list above with his unit numbers stated.

### B12. Chapter 20 as a whole (pp. 1728-29, text): grand introversion
Concerns: 20:1-27.
Facts: A worship of chthonic gods (Molek and necromancy) 1-6; B sanctification 7; C exhortation to obey 8; **X penalties for violation 9-21**; C' obey 22-25; B' sanctification 26; A' necromancy 27. Shared vocabulary A/A' (mot yumat; stoning); B/B' "you shall be holy, for I am holy"; C/C' "keep my laws"; fourfold hibdil (separate) in 24-26. Alternative (Wenham): two subjects, Molek and sex, each opening "any man" (2, 9), closing with holiness (7-8, 26); or (Dillmann, Hartley): sex 9-21 enclosed by exhortations and two stoning crimes (2, 27). Chapters 18-20 together: **ABXB'A'** with 19 at the centre (B12 → A1).
Feeds: **unit map / peaks** (19 flanked by 18 and 20). Text only.

### B13. 20:9-21: the penalty ladder, and Hildenbrand's introversion (pp. 1742-44, text)

### B14. Chapters 21-22 as one block (pp. 1792-93, text)
Concerns: 21:1-22:33.
Facts: Hildenbrand's thematic introversion: A priest and his family in relation to the sacrifice (21:1-15); B blemishes of priests (21:16-23); **X how a priest avoids desecrating the sacrifices (22:1-16)**; B' blemishes of animals (22:17-25); A' an animal and its family (22:27-28). The centre merges priests and animals in "sacrificial food" and is the only part stating penalties (karet 22:3; bearing sin 22:9; reparation 22:16). Six sections end with "who sanctifies" (meqaddes + suffix): 21:8, 15, 23; 22:9, 16, 32 (four inner objects refer to priests; two outer to Israel). Douglas's observation: the order reverses chs. 1-7 (priests first, then animals). Number-seven devices: qds seven times in 21:6-8; "Israel" seven times in 22; "acceptable" (rason) seven times in 22. Common denominator: "blemishes" (mourning marks, wrong marriage, visible defects, eating while impure, wrong eaters).
Feeds: **unit map**, **tabernacle map layer** (who may enter or eat), **qadash**. Text only.

### B15. Chapter 21 (pp. 1794-95, 1798-1801, text): content outline and the priest/high-priest rules
Concerns: 21:1-24.
Facts (outline in Milgrom's order): A. priests (1-15 introduced by "say ... and say"): mourning ban (1b-6; exceptions mother, father, son, daughter, brother, and the unwed sister "closest to him" 2-3; no bald patches, no beard-shaving, no gashes 5; holiness 6); prohibited marriages (7-8: promiscuous or raped woman, divorced woman); addendum, priest's daughter who whores is burned (9). B. high priest (10-15): no dishevelled hair or torn vestments (10); no approach to any corpse, even his parents (11); may not leave the sacred area (12); marriage only to a young virgin of his kin; **widow, divorcee, raped woman, harlot all barred** (13-14); reason: not to profane his offspring (15). C. blemishes (16-22): see B17. D. blemished high priest (23). E. Moses reports (24).
Gradation: ordinary priest (may bury 5 kin, may marry a virgin or a widow), high priest (no corpse at all, no widow); blemished priest (may eat sacred food, "most holy and holy," 22, but may not enter before the veil or serve at the altar, 23). The wife is not on the list of permitted burials (Milgrom takes se'er as blood relatives, against Rabbi Akiba, who put her in).
Feeds: **condensed table** (priest / high priest / blemished priest: can bury, can marry, can eat, can enter), **tabernacle map layer** (the veil threshold), **ritual**. Text only; check the verse placement of "virgin sister" against Milgrom's NOTE on 21:3.

### B16. Chapter 22 (pp. 1845-47, text): who may eat what, then the animals
Concerns: 22:1-33.
Facts: three sections, each opened by its own divine address (1-2; 17-18a; 26). **I. Priests (1-16)**: impure priests must not touch or eat sacred donations (karet, 3); sources of impurity listed in 4; touching and washing (5-7); no carrion (8); **non-priests (10-16)**: a layman may not eat sacred food; so also a priest's resident hireling (10b); the priest's purchased slave may (11); a widowed or divorced childless priest's daughter may (12-13a); inadvertent lay eater adds 20 percent and a reparation (14-16). **II. Blemished animals (17-25)**: burnt offerings 18b-20; well-being offerings 21-24 (blemishes 22-24, see B17); none from foreigners (25). **III. More rules for animals (26-33)**: mother and young (27-28), thanksgiving offering eaten the same day (29-30), closing exhortation 31-33.
Feeds: **tabernacle map layer** (who may eat sacred food), **ritual**, **condensed table**. Text only.

### B17. The twelve blemishes: priests (21:17-21) against animals (22:22-24) (pp. 1836-38, 1876-77, 1826, text)
Concerns: 21:17-21 and 22:22-24 (and 21:17b-21 as a big AXA' structure).
Facts: priestly twelve (Milgrom's translations): blind, lame, disfigured (face; hapax), deformed (long limb), broken leg, broken arm (two items), hunchback, dwarf (thin), a growth or discoloration of the eye, scar (scabies), lichen, crushed testicle. Grouping: **4, 2, 6**. Animal twelve: blind, broken, maimed, with a growth (wen), scabbed, scarred; (two: overlong or stunted limb); four kinds of damaged testes: **6, 2, 4**. Milgrom: the lists are arranged chiastically and are artificial (both "twelve"); "five identical items" (blind, overgrown limb, broken bones, sores, scab); common test = the defect is visible to anyone, the one exception being the testicle, added to match. The rabbis expand to 142 blemishes; the number twelve is generic, not exhaustive.
Structure of 21:17b-21: AXA' with "blemish" (mum) always at the centre (summit of H's art; see vol. 1 p. 40-42); each of the two panels and two chiasms keeps mum in the middle; the final chiasm (21:21b) runs C/D/X/D'/C'. The animal passage (22:22-24) is AXA' in three panels of three lines; the middle (v. 23, overgrown or stunted limb may be a freewill offering but not a vow) is the only one allowing any offering for a blemished animal.
Feeds: **condensed table** (the 12 against 12 matched), **tabernacle map layer** (who is excluded), **qadash**. Uncertainty: Milgrom concedes many Hebrew terms are unidentifiable (and the page image of the matching is only in text); the grouping 4/2/6 vs 6/2/4 comes from the text (p. 1876), not from a figure.

### B18. The four states: holy / common / pure / impure (pp. 1720-21, text; a small diagram)
Concerns: the concept behind 10:10, 11:44-47, 20:25.
Facts: a box diagram in which holy and common are adjacent to pure and impure (holy and common can be pure; common can be pure or impure) but **holy and impure never touch**. Holy and impure are "dynamic and contagious" (they extend over common and pure); common and pure are "static." The boundaries are permeable (dashed): Israel is to push the holy into the common and shrink the impure, not the other way. The impurity table (death, blood, semen, scale disease) all stand for death; holiness stands for life. The ch. 22 gradation fits: holy vs impure must never meet.
Feeds: **qadash** (the key diagram), **tabernacle map layer** (what may enter), **ritual**. **Check page image (pdf 479)**: I read the diagram only from Milgrom's description; the converter did not flag it.

### B19. Frames and inclusions across chs. 18-22 (pp. 1323-24, text): for the unit map
Concerns: the inclusions that bind units.
Facts: 18:2, 30 enclose ch. 18 (YHWH's Sinai-style self-introduction); 19:36b-37 (exodus rationale) rounds off 18:2b-5; 20:8, 22 enclose 20:9-21, and 20:22b repeats 18:28aa verbatim, enclosing 18 and 20 together; 21:1, 4 (priests); 22:2, 32 bind ch. 22 and equate Israelites with priests in guarding the holy (abcd/b'a'd'c'); **22:29-30 with 19:5-6** envelop chs. 19-22 (the thanksgiving offering moved from the well-being law to ch. 22 to create this). Also 18:24b-26 (ABB'A' on four "separate" verbs: God separates Israel from the nations; Israel separates clean from unclean animals) and 18:24-30 (two six-part panels: the Canaanites' fate, then Israel's if it follows).
Feeds: **unit map / peaks** (what sits inside what), **qadash** (separation = holiness, p. 1762: "holy" means both separate-from and separate-to). Text only.

## Places where Milgrom's reconstruction differs from one he discusses
- **Ring of Leviticus (A1)**: Douglas's diagram, "slightly amended"; he agrees with the ring but disputes her terminology, and extends the centre to the whole Torah.
- **Lev 19 structure (B10, A3)**: against Magonet (three parts) he reads AXA'; against Crüsemann/Ruwe a forced introversion; against Schwartz he keeps 33-36 as an appendix but counts 18 units; his own text switches between 14, 16 and 18 units.
- **Lev 19 and the Decalogue (A4)**: rejects the "dodecalogue" and a full ten-for-ten match; keeps only commandments 1, 2, 4, 5 (plus the prologue).
- **Lev 18 / 20 (B11, B13)**: not one law but two independent lists, against those who see 20 as continuing 18; rejects Hildenbrand's introversion of 20:10-21 as meaningless; rejects Daube's three-stage growth and Douglas's alternation; rejects Carmichael's Genesis-story derivation and Meacham's apology-for-the-patriarchs.
- **Rattray (A2)**: adopted for the inner circle; Schwartz's view of v. 6 as a heading for the whole list is rejected.
- **Chs. 21-22 (B14)**: Hildenbrand's introversion adopted; Wellhausen's late dating from the missing "tent of meeting" rejected; blemishes: the Qumran and rabbinic readings (deaf, 142 items) are kept as comparison only.
