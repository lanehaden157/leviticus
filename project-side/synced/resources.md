# Leviticus Study — Resources

Read before pass 1 of every unit. Start, not limit. Don't stretch a listed
source past its actual content. Edit in the repo, never on the project side.

All four files are text conversions with page markers: `[p. N]` is the printed page (cite it), `[pdf p. N]` where there is none. `[figure …]` / `[table …]` mark a figure the text lost; the repo's `figures-*.md` catalogue says what each one shows. The text holds native Hebrew script in places: never copy it into chat or an artifact; transliterate and gloss.

## Jacob Milgrom, *Leviticus 1–16* (Anchor Bible 3, 1991)
File: `milgrom-lev1-16.txt`

The standard critical commentary on the sacrificial and purity laws: translation, NOTES verse by verse, and long COMMENTS. The primary source for the offerings (ch. 1–7), the priests' ordination (8–10), the food and impurity laws (11–15) and the Day of Atonement (16). Milgrom's theory that sin and impurity pollute the sanctuary, which the ḥaṭṭāʾt purges, drives his reading of kipper and ḥaṭṭāʾt. Weigh it heavily on ritual procedure and terms, but say when a reading depends on that theory, or on his P/H source division, rather than on the text. Figures: `figures-milgrom-1.md`.

## Jacob Milgrom, *Leviticus 17–22* (Anchor Bible 3A, 2000)
File: `milgrom-lev17-22.txt`

Covers the Holiness Code's first half: blood and slaughter (17), forbidden unions (18, 20), the holiness chapter (19), and the priests' and offerings' fitness (21–22). Also holds his introduction to H and a full-book translation. The source for qadash in H's sense (holiness extended to all Israel and the land). Its H-redaction arguments are a fact about the field, not a verdict. Figures: `figures-milgrom-2.md`.

## Jacob Milgrom, *Leviticus 23–27* (Anchor Bible 3B, 2001)
File: `milgrom-lev23-27.txt`

The calendar (23), the lamp and bread and the blasphemer (24), sabbatical and jubilee with redemption and the goel (25), blessings and curses (26), and vows and dedications (27). Strong comparisons with Num 28–29, Deut 15–16 and Deut 28. Figures: `figures-milgrom-3.md`.

**Only one Milgrom volume is in project knowledge at a time:** 1–16 for units in Lev 1–16, 17–22 for 17–22, 23–27 for 23–27. Before pass 1, check that the unit's volume is there. If it, or a volume a cross-reference points to, is missing, say so and don't work from memory of Milgrom.

**Milgrom's transliteration is OCR-damaged in all three volumes** (`hatta^t`, `l;`, `$`, `-fm`, etc.). Read it with `milgrom-transliteration-key.md`, and never quote a transliteration from these files without checking it.

## L. Michael Morales, *Who Shall Ascend the Mountain of the Lord? A Biblical Theology of the Book of Leviticus* (NSBT 37, 2015)
File: `morales-who-shall-ascend.txt`

A canonical biblical theology, not a commentary. The book as an ascent into God's presence, centred on the Day of Atonement, with Eden, Sinai, tabernacle and temple read as one mountain-of-God pattern. Good for the book's structure and the peaks, for the tabernacle's graded zones, and for canon threads and type-scenes. Evangelical; reads toward the NT in its last chapters. Use it for structure and theology, never for philology or ritual detail. Markers are pdf pages (ebook, no printed pages). Endnotes are gathered at the end by chapter. Figures: `figures-morales.md`.

## Gaps: not on hand

The commentaries above are the full set; no more are coming. The ones below are reachable only by web search in pass 2.

Wenham (NICOT), Sklar (TOTC), Hartley (WBC), Gane (NIVAC), Rashi and Ibn Ezra on Leviticus. Milgrom's one-volume Continental Commentary is also absent, but the three Anchor volumes cover it. Flag any unit where a second full commentary, especially a non-source-critical one like Wenham, would materially change the reading; search the web for it in pass 2 and say what you read.
