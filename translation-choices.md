# Translation Choices

A working glossary of deliberate English renderings for this study's own
translation. **Starts from `canon-conventions.md`** (a vendored copy of `bible-core/canon/conventions.md`; don't edit it here): every row
there is this book's default too (y'all, Yahweh, sky/skies, slave, life/being,
look, chest, covenant, devote to destruction, inheritance, ḥesed untranslated,
…). This file records only what Leviticus decides for itself, including any
deviation from a canon default and the reason.

## How to use this file

Before rendering a word in a new unit, check here and in the canon conventions
and match a prior decision. If a different rendering fits better in one verse,
use it and flag the deviation; Lane decides whether it's a one-off or a
correction. A chat records a decision in the handoff's "Decisions"; Claude Code
updates the row and the Log. If a unit and this file disagree, flag it: the
unit is more likely right.

## Open terms (for Lane)

*Tentative draft: options, not decisions. Counts come from `Leviticus-words.tsv`, which
uses Hebrew chapter numbers; clusters below are English chapters, heaviest first (Eng
6:1-7 and 6:8-30 sit under Heb 5 and 6). Some counts include non-technical uses of the
same word. Plural "you" is always y'all.*

### 1. kipper (kaphar, Strong's 3722; 49 uses)
Clusters: 16 (16 uses), 14 (7), 5 (6), 4, 8-10, 12, 15, 17, 19, 23.
- **make atonement**: the traditional reading; familiar and short. It brings in an idea of reconciliation that the verb lacks when its object is a thing (an altar, a house, 14:53).
- **purge / cleanse**: Milgrom's line: the rite removes contamination from the sanctuary and people. It fits altars and houses, and reads oddly for "atone for his sin" (4:26).
- **cover**: the sense some link to Akkadian or Arabic cognates. It keeps a literal image, but is not clearly the sense in these verses.
- **kipper** (transliterated): one fixed word for all objects, followable across 4, 14 and 16. It costs readability.

### 2. ḥaṭṭaʾt (2403, 2398; about 110 uses with the verb)
Clusters: 4-5 (about 55), 16 (14), 9, 10, 14, 6, 12, 19, 26. The same word means "sin" (4:3, 16:16) and the offering.
- **sin offering**: the traditional reading; keeps the link to "sin" in every verse.
- **purification offering**: Milgrom's reading, from the blood that purges the altar. It hides the "sin" link, and the offering is also brought after birth (12:6-8).
- **cleansing offering**: a plainer cousin of the above; same gain and cost.
- **ḥaṭṭaʾt** (transliterated): keeps both senses together, as in 4:3. Opaque on first read.

### 3. ʾasham (817; 27 uses)
Clusters: 5 and 6:1-7 (9), 14 (9), 7 (5), 19 (3).
- **guilt offering**: the traditional reading; works with the "he is guilty" verb in 5:2-5. It misses the repayment of loss.
- **reparation offering**: stresses the compensation plus a fifth in 5:16 and 6:5. Less natural for the cleansed person's ʾasham in 14.
- **compensation offering**: plainer English than "reparation"; same strength and weakness.
- **ʾasham** (transliterated): lets chapter 14 and chapter 5 sit together without a decision. Opaque.

### 4. ʿolah (5930; 62 uses)
Clusters: 4 and 9 (10 each), 1 (8), 6, 14, 7, 8, 12, 16, 23.
- **burnt offering**: traditional and clear. It stresses the burning rather than what the word says ("goes up").
- **ascending offering**: tracks the root ʿalah, "go up", and the smoke rising (1:9, 13, 17). Unfamiliar English.
- **whole offering**: highlights that all but the hide goes up (1:9, 13). It can be mistaken for the "wholly" language of other rites.

### 5. qorban (7133; 40 uses)
Clusters: 2 (8), 3 (7), 1 and 7 (6 each), then 4, 9, 22, 27, 17, 23. Linked to the verb hiqriv, "bring near".
- **offering**: simple, but the study also needs it for the named offerings, so the distinction is lost.
- **near-offering / what is brought near**: shows the root link (1:2, 3, 5) and keeps qorban apart from the named offerings. Awkward.
- **gift brought near**: warmer; shows it as given to Yahweh. It imports an interpretation (gift).
- **qorban** (transliterated): the word returns in later Jewish and Gospel texts (Mark 7:11), so a reader can follow it. It stays foreign in English.

### 6. ṭameʾ / ṭahor (2930-2932, 2889/2891; about 150 and 65 uses)
Clusters: ṭameʾ 15 (about 36), 11 (34), 13 (23), 18, 5, 22, 7, 14; ṭahor 14 (21), 13 (17), 11, 12, 15, 16.
- **unclean / clean**: traditional. It can be heard as dirt or sin, which ritual uncleanness is not.
- **impure / pure**: more abstract; fits sanctuary contamination (15:31, 16:16). It can sound moral.
- **contaminated / decontaminated**: stresses the spreading, contagious nature. It has a clinical sound.
- **ṭameʾ / ṭahor** (transliterated): avoids "unclean" being heard as sin or dirt. Heavy across 200 uses.

### 7. tsaraʿat (6883; 29 uses)
Clusters: 13 (21), 14 (8). Applied to skin, cloth, leather and houses.
- **skin disease**: familiar and medical-sounding; strains for cloth and houses (13:47, 14:34).
- **leprosy**: the traditional rendering, though Hansen's disease is not what is described.
- **scale disease**: a neutral coinage from the scaly look; also awkward for houses.
- **tsaraʿat** (transliterated): fits skin, cloth and houses alike. It leaves the reader with no picture.

### 8. karet (karat, 3772; about 20 uses as the penalty)
Clusters: 20 (5), 7 and 17 (4 each), 22, 26, 18, 19, 23. Includes a few plain "cut" uses.
- **cut off from his people**: literal; leaves open whether it means death, exile or loss of line, which the text does not say.
- **be cut off** (short): shorter; drops "from his people". Equally unexplained.
- **karet** (transliterated; the rabbinic name): treats it as one technical penalty that the study can discuss. Imposes a later category.

### 9. qadash refinement (6942, 6918, 6944; about 140 uses)
Clusters: 27 and 22 (about 20 each), 23, 16, 10, 21, 20, 6, 8. The canon default is "set apart" for the reflexive/consecrate sense and "holy" for adjective and noun (`canon-conventions.md`); a holiness-heavy book may refine it.
- **keep the canon default**: "holy" for adjective and noun, "set apart" for the verb. Consistent across books, but hides the root link in 20:7-8 and 22:32.
- **holy / make holy (one family)**: one English family for all three, so "be holy, for I am holy" (19:2) and "I am Yahweh who makes you holy" (20:8) read as one root. Stiff in 8:10-12, where objects are anointed.
- **holy / consecrate / the holy thing**: a separate verb sense for rites (8:10-12) and a noun for qodesh ("holy portions", 22). Clearer rites, lost root link.

### 10. goʾel (1350, 1353; 31 uses with geʾullah)
Clusters: 25 (about 19), 27 (12). Chapter 25 uses it for the next of kin who buys back land or kin; 27 for redeeming devoted things.
- **redeemer**: fits the canon default and later prophetic and New Testament use. Heavy for a family duty about land.
- **kinsman-redeemer**: shows the family tie; the traditional pairing. Clumsy, and does not fit 27.
- **next-of-kin who buys back**: plain; explains 25:25-26. Wordy at every use.
- **goʾel** (transliterated): lets Numbers 35's avenger and Leviticus 25's redeemer be seen as one role (`canon-conventions.md`). Opaque.
