### 2. Fig. 1, p. 135 (pdf 159): Ground plan of the Tabernacle
Concerns: Exod 25-27, 36-38; Lev 1-16 setting (comment on the sacrificial system). Page is rotated 90 degrees.
Shows: a rectangular court with an entrance gap on one short side, the shrine in the western half, the altar and laver in the court. Posts drawn as dots; dashed diagonals only mark the layout.
Facts (cubits; read from the figure):
| Item | Size / position |
|---|---|
| Outer court | 100 long x 50 wide; entrance 20 wide on the short (east) side |
| Altar of burnt offering | 5 x 5, on the central axis in the court, nearer the entrance |
| Laver | round, on the axis between the altar and the shrine |
| Shrine (tent) | in the west half; outer shrine about 20 long, adytum about 10, width 10 (total 30 x 10) |
| Inside the outer shrine | table (about 2 x 1), menorah (round), altar of incense (about 1 x 1) |
| Adytum | Ark about 2.5 x 1.5 |
Zones that follow: court (open to Israelites to the altar, per Milgrom), shrine (priests), adytum (high priest once a year, ch. 16).
Feeds: **tabernacle map** (base geometry and the layers court / holy place / most holy place). Check page image: the shrine's 20/10 labels vs. total length (labels show 20 beside the table and 10 beside the Ark); the north arrow is rotated.

### 3. Fig. 2a-c, p. 206 (pdf 230): Ox anatomy photos
Concerns: 3:3-4, 9-10, 14-15 (the suet/fat parts). Three plates: (a) stomachs, paunch, caul (great omentum), spleen; (b) suet about the entrails: mesentery, intestines, lesser omentum, liver, gall bladder; (c) suet about the kidneys, with paunch and omasum.
Facts: only labels naming the organs; the key points live in the text (which fat is offered: the fat covering the entrails, the two kidneys with fat on them, the lobe of the liver, per Milgrom's own discussion, not in the figure).
Feeds: **ritual** (well-being offering: parts to the altar), Leviticus term entries for suet/fat. Photos not reusable; text-only use. Photo fidelity poor; check page image if you need the labels.

### 4. Fig. 3, p. 209 (pdf 233): Clay model of a liver
Concerns: 3:4, 10, 15 (the liver lobe, "the appendage on the liver"). A Mesopotamian clay liver model used for divination; Milgrom ties the "lobe" (yoteret) to liver-reading. Photograph.
Feeds: Leviticus term (lobe/appendage on the liver) only. Not otherwise useful.

### 5. Fig. 4, p. 212 (pdf 236): Broadtail sheep with a cart
Concerns: 3:9 (the whole fat tail offered with the sheep). Photo of a Palestinian sheep with its tail supported by a small cart.
Facts: the fat tail is large and counts as one of the offered parts for a sheep (not for goats or cattle).
Feeds: **ritual** (well-being offering of a sheep: tail goes up), term "fat tail". Text only.

### 6. Fig. 5a-b, p. 235 (pdf 259): Horned altars
Concerns: 4:7, 18, 25, 30 (horns of the altar). (a) A horned incense altar from Megiddo; (b) the reconstructed horned altar from Beer-sheba. Photographs.
Feeds: **tabernacle map** (illustration for the horned altar of burnt offering and the altar of incense); Leviticus term "horns of the altar". Image reuse is a copyright matter; use as reference only.

### 7. Fig. 6, p. 258 (pdf 282): Which sin reaches how far into the sanctuary
Concerns: ch. 4 (purification offering), ch. 16. Milgrom's Comment B on how sin pollutes.
Shows: a schematic of the sanctuary (outer court with the altar, the shrine with the incense altar, the adytum with the Ark) with three arrows from categories of sin to the place they reach.
Facts:
| Sin type | Where its pollution lodges |
|---|---|
| Individual, involuntary (chap. 4 individual) | the outer altar (blood on the horns of the altar of burnt offering) |
| Communal, involuntary (priest or whole community) | the shrine: the incense altar and the veil |
| Brazen and unrepented offences | the adytum: the Ark's cover (kapporet), purged only on the Day of Purgation |
Rule shown: the worse the sin, the deeper the pollution travels.
Feeds: **tabernacle map layer "who/what pollutes where"**, the **ritual** step strips for ch. 4 and ch. 16 (where the blood goes). Strongest single diagram for Leviticus 4 and 16 together.

### 8. Table, p. 354 (pdf 378): Hittite penalties for violating holy things
Concerns: ch. 5 (sacrilege, trespass on the holy). Four tiers of penalty (death by the gods, death by man for all, death by man for the criminal only, less than death), with columns: text, criminal, crime, place. Examples of "crimes": taking sacrificial animals, changing the rite's timing, dividing the offering portions, approaching the god's bread unclean.
Facts: a ladder of penalties from divine death through human execution to fines/repeating the rite. Used only for contrast with Israel's reparation offering.
Feeds: **lawparallel** (Hittite comparison for 5:14-26, reparation vs. death), low priority. Hittite: location of rows needs the page image if wanted.

## B. Chapters 8-10: priestly consecration, elevation, ephod

### 9. Fig. 7, p. 431 (pdf 455): Ox skeleton
Concerns: 7:30-34 (the breast and the right thigh). Unlabelled skeleton of a bovine; used with a butcher's note on how an animal is quartered so that the breast/brisket comes away as one piece and the hind leg gives the right thigh.
Facts: breast (brisket) = between the forelegs, from neck to flank; right thigh = hindquarter cut at the loins; foreleg separate.
Feeds: **ritual** for the well-being offering (who gets what): breast and right thigh to the priests, suet to the altar. Converter missed this one.

### 10. Fig. 8, p. 451 (pdf 475): Two concentric temple plans (P's tabernacle and Ezekiel's temple)
Concerns: 7:19-21 and Comment on contagion of the holy; Ezek 40-46.
Shows: two square outlines. Left: the Priestly pattern (the laity reach the forecourt as far as the altar). Right: Ezekiel's temple, labelled A (inner court), B (north gate, slaughter), B' (east gate), C (outer court).
Facts (Ezekiel, from the text on p. 452):
| Zone | Who has access |
|---|---|
| Inner court A, altar, temple buildings | priests |
| North gate of the inner court B (slaughter) | Levites |
| East gate B' | the prince (nasi) |
| Outer court C | laity only here |
P: laity may enter the court up to the altar and help with the sacrifice; Ezekiel bars them from the inner court and has Levites slaughter and cook.
Feeds: **tabernacle map "who may enter" layer** (and the contrast with Ezekiel). Caption continues on p. 452 (three domains corresponding to priests, Levites, laity).

### 11. Fig. 9, p. 471 (pdf 495): Elevation rite on a relief
Concerns: 7:30 (tenupa, "elevation offering"). Photo of an Egyptian relief from Karnak: a king raising offerings before a god. Supports Milgrom's reading: the offering is lifted, not waved to and fro.
Feeds: Leviticus term "elevation offering" (tenupa) only; **ritual** step (priest raises the breast). Photo; text carries the information.

### 12. Hittite ritual inventory/performance table, pp. 496-497 (pdf 520-521)
Concerns: ch. 8 (consecration). Two-page table pairing an inventory list with a performance list, numbered 1-50 (the list of items: animals, clothes, loaves, clay figures, wax figures, etc.), in an Anatolian purification/transfer ritual. Milgrom uses it to argue that Leviticus 8's inventory (8:2) and performance sections are paired in the same way.
Facts: each numbered item in the inventory reappears in the performance, in a partly different order; items marked uncertain with "?". A footnote on p. 497 notes a short subsection whose order does not match.
Feeds: Leviticus 8 (inventory vs. performance structure) as a note; **ritual** only indirectly. Low priority. Check page image if details wanted (numbers are visible, ritual's name is not on these pages).

### 13. Fig. 10, p. 503 (pdf 527): Egyptian broad collar
Concerns: 8:7-8, Exod 28 (ephod, breastpiece). Drawing of a wide Egyptian collar with falcon-head clasps and teardrop pendants. Parallel for priestly adornment; no structure.

### 14. Fig. 11, p. 506 (pdf 530): Reconstructed high priest's vestments
Concerns: 8:7-9, Exod 28. Line drawing of the ephod over a robe with the square breastpiece (12 stones in 4 rows of 3) and the hem with bells and pomegranates.
Facts: breastpiece = 4 rows x 3 stones (12), tunic hem with bells.
Feeds: Leviticus term "ephod", "breastpiece" (Urim and Thummim context). Reference image only.

### 15. Comparative table, p. 514 (pdf 538): Anointing in Exod 29:7, Exod 40:9-13, Lev 8:10-12
Concerns: 8:10-12. Three columns of Hebrew (transliterated) lined up. Shows the order of anointing: Exod 29 has only the oil poured on Aaron's head; Exod 40 anoints the tabernacle, its furnishings, the altar, laver and Aaron; Lev 8 anoints the tabernacle, sprinkles the altar seven times, anoints altar/laver, then pours oil on Aaron. A dashed arrow marks the altar anointing that Lev 8 moves.
Facts: sequence in Lev 8:10-12: (1) Moses takes the anointing oil; (2) anoints the tabernacle and all in it, consecrating it; (3) sprinkles the altar seven times; (4) anoints altar, utensils, laver and stand; (5) pours oil on Aaron's head. Used by Milgrom to argue that vv. 10-11 are later.
Feeds: **ritual** for Lev 8 (the consecration sequence, who does what). Hebrew parts only a source for the order.

### 16. Fig. 12, p. 566 (pdf 590): Rite of passage diagram
Concerns: ch. 8-9 (priestly consecration as a rite of passage). Diagram (after van Gennep/Leach): initial normal condition, then a rite of separation, then a marginal state (outside society and time), then a rite of aggregation, then the final normal condition in a new status.
Facts: three phases, separation, margin, aggregation. Milgrom uses it to read the priests' consecration (the seven days at the tent entrance, 8:33-35, is the natural candidate for the marginal phase; check the surrounding text before stating this).
Feeds: **ritual** (Lev 8 as phases) and a Lev 8 unit map; "rite of passage" term.

### 20. Unnumbered diagram, p. 616 (pdf 640): Sacred / common / pure / impure
Concerns: 10:10-11 (priests distinguish holy from common and impure from pure). Two overlapping bars: sacred -> common, and pure -> impure, with dotted lines marking that the boundaries move.

### 21. Table, pp. 825-826 (pdf 849-850): Wright's chart of skin-disease diagnosis (13:2-44)
Concerns: 13:2-44. Rows grouped A-G by type of lesion, with verses, colour, hair/depth, first and second quarantine (7 days each), result, final verdict (pure or impure).
Facts (summary; the full chart is long):
- A (2-8) baheret, a white spot, not deep, no white hair: quarantine 7 days; if it spread = impure; faded = pure; same = quarantine again; second quarantine: spread = impure, faded = pure, same = pure (washes clothes, v. 6).
- B (9-17) rising or swelling (se'et), white, with white hair and raw flesh = impure; if the whole body has turned white = pure.
- C (18-23) boil healed, then a white or reddish-white mark: impure if hair white and deep; faint = quarantine 7 days; spread = impure; same = pure.
- D (24-28) burn mark: same pattern as C.
- E (29-37) head or beard scall (neqa'), yellow thin hair and deep = impure; otherwise quarantine, shaving (not the scall) and a second quarantine; black hair growing = pure.
- F (38-39) white spots on skin: faint = pure.
- G (40-44) baldness alone = pure; red-white lesion on a bald area like scale disease = impure.
Sigla key at the foot: B black hair, D deep, W white hair, Y yellow hair, R raw flesh, Q quarantine, S shaving, + present, - lacking, [] deduction.
Feeds: **condensed table for Lev 13**, **ritual** for Lev 13 as a flowchart (who examines, when). Written by David Wright, not Milgrom.
Check page image: yes, if you build a flowchart; the table has many dittos.

### 23. Table F "Purification procedures and effects", pp. 986-991 (pdf 1010-1015)
Concerns: chs. 12-15, Num 19 (comment on genital discharges). The single most useful table in the book. Three parts across six pages.
Part I (pp. 986-987): twelve impurity bearers, six "major" (ended by a sacrifice) and six "minor" (ended at evening), with duration and stages. Part II (pp. 988-989): effect on the common (the camp, family, sex, contact). Part III (pp. 990-991): effect on the sacred (food, sanctuary).
Facts, Part I (days; remedy; sacrifice):
| # | Bearer | Duration | Steps in order | Final sacrifice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | scale-diseased person (Lev 14) | x + 7 (8) | open-ended wait; day 1: sprinkle, launder, shave, bathe; day 7: shave, launder, bathe; day 8 | purification offering (ewe or bird) + 3 other sacrifices + 2 daubings |
| 2 | parturient (Lev 12) | 7 (14) + 33 (66) | 7 (14) days; day 7 (14): launder, bathe; day 41 (81) | purification offering (bird) + lamb/bird |
| 3 | zab (15:3-15, 28-30) | x + 7 (8) | wait; 7 days; day 7: launder, bathe; day 8 | purification offering (bird) + a bird |
| 4 | priest contaminated by a corpse (Ezek 44:26-27) | 7 + 7 (8) | 7 days; sprinkling on days 3 and 7 [deduced]; launder, bathe; day 15 | purification offering (a bird?) |
| 5 | Nazirite contaminated by a corpse (Num 6:9-12) | 7 (8) | 7 days; sprinkling on days 3 and 7 [deduced]; shave; day 8 | purification offering + bird + bird + lamb |
| 6 | person whose impurity is accidentally prolonged (5:1-13) | x + 1 | wait; day x: bathe; next day | purification offering (ewe/bird/semolina) |
| 7 | lay person contaminated by a corpse (Num 5:2; 19) | 7 | sprinkling on days 3 and 7; launder, bathe day 7; evening | none |
| 8 | menstruant (15:19-24) | 7 | day 7: launder, bathe [deduced]; evening | none |
| 9 | handler of red cow, scapegoat, burnt purification offering | 1 | pre-ablution, then launder and bathe; evening | none |
| 10 | emission of semen (15:16-18) | 1 | pre-ablution, launder, bathe; evening | none |
| 11 | carcass contamination (11:24-40; 22:5) | 1 | pre-ablution, bathe (launder); evening | none |
| 12 | secondarily contaminated (15; 22:4b-7; Num 19) | 1 | pre-ablution, bathe (launder); evening | none |
Part II (effect on the common) and III (effect on the sacred), by row; "direct" = contact, "airborne" = through the air of the camp:
- Rows 1-6 (major): all stay away from sacred things and food, and must bring a sacrifice; they are airborne polluters of the sanctuary (hence the purification offering).
- Scale-diseased: kept outside the camp; others under the same roof may not enter the sanctuary.
- Parturient, zab, corpse-contaminated: at home with restrictions (zab washes hands to touch others); in P2/H (later or stricter layer) the zab and the corpse-contaminated are banished from the camp (Num 5).
- Minor rows 7-12: little or no effect on the common after the stated rite; sacred contact barred until evening or until laundering and bathing; row 9 handler stays outside the camp; row 11-12 even priests may eat holy things after sundown (22:4b-7).
Milgrom notes two layers (P1 and P2/H) differ on whether they banish (P2/H) or allow staying at home (P1); the table gives both.
Feeds: **condensed table for Lev 12-15**, **ritual** (the eighth-day sequence for scale disease and discharges), **calendar** (day counts: 7, 8, 14, 33, 66), and the **Leviticus terms** (zab, menstruant, purification offering). First priority for Wave 2. Check page images pp. 988-991 before building: parts II and III are in small cells with "[direct]" and "[airborne]" marked as deductions.

## Where Milgrom's diagrams differ from other reconstructions
- **Access to the court (Fig. 8):** Milgrom's P tabernacle lets the layman enter the court up to the altar. Ezekiel's plan bars the laity from the inner court and the north gate; the figure puts the two side by side.
- **Banishment vs. staying at home (Table F):** Milgrom shows two layers: P1 lets the zab, parturient, and corpse-contaminated stay at home, while P2/H (Num 5) banishes them from the camp.
- **Wright's trees (Figs. 15-19):** Milgrom corrects Wright's earlier trees: the zab's bed keeps impurity until day 7 (x, not x+1), the menstruant's touch is harmless if she washed her hands, the zabah follows the Greek of 15:27, and the seat/saddle branch changes. Do not use the uncorrected trees.
- **"Qodes" in ch. 16 (pp. 1013-14 text):** here it means the adytum; elsewhere in P it means the outer shrine. Our map should label the zones by chapter.
- **Measures (p. 894):** Milgrom's cor of 120 kabs and lethech of 60 kabs are marked tentative; the Talmud gives other ratios (lethech = half a cor = 90 kabs).
