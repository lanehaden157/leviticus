### 8. Table 2 and Table 3, p. 2182 (pdf 312): sabbatical-year farming calendars (viewed)
Concerns: 25:20-22 (the "three-year" harvest promise). 
Shows: two small year-grids with fall (sow) and spring (reap) columns.
Facts:
| Table | Calendar assumed | Year 6 | Year 7 (sabbatical) | Year 8 | Year 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Table 2 (Rashi) | fall for both religion and farming | fall sow / spring reap | rest | fall sow, spring reap | fall sow, spring reap |
| Table 3 (Morgenstern) | spring | spring reap; fall nothing | rest both seasons | fall sow | fall sow; spring reap |
Milgrom rejects both: on Rashi, the sixth-year harvest cannot last through year 9; on Morgenstern, sowing in year 6 and tending the crop is possible in a sabbatical (growing crop may be eaten, vv. 11-12), and the jubilee starts in the seventh month, so the fall calendar governs.
Feeds: **jubilee mechanics** (one-page "sabbatical/jubilee in time"); optional condensed table. **Check page image**.

### 9. Table 4, p. 2183 (pdf 313): Ramban's calendar for sabbatical and jubilee together (viewed; missed by converter)
Concerns: 25:20-22.
Shows: fall and spring columns with years 5-9 and a boxed two-year rest.
Facts: sow (fall) in year 5, reap (spring) year 6; year 6 fall and year 7 spring = sabbatical; year 7 fall and year 8 spring = jubilee; sow fall of year 8, reap spring year 9. Rest of two consecutive years fits "crop of the sixth year lasts three years". The years should be numbered 48, 49, 50, 51 (the 50th year is the jubilee; no year 51 in the pentecontad system, so the notation was probably shortened).
Milgrom: this is "the one that works best", with the proviso that sabbatical and jubilee follow the fall (agricultural) calendar while festivals follow spring. 
Feeds: **jubilee mechanics**, calendar (sabbatical and jubilee start in the seventh month).

### 10. Figure 2, p. 2249 (pdf 379): rotational fallowing with a sabbatical (viewed)
Concerns: 25:2-7, 20-22, answering "how can a farmer leave land idle?" (Comment C).
Shows: two plots, P and Q, across two 7-year cycles. Letters: C crop, F fallow, S sabbatical (rest). Boxes mark runs of two cropped years before an S.
Facts: the two plots alternate crop and fallow every year; in the year before the sabbatical the plot that would have rested is cropped instead (two crops in a row), then rests through the sabbatical year and the following year; the plots swap roles in the second cycle. Credit: M. Hildenbrand's note and Hopkins (1985) on biennial fallow.
Feeds: **jubilee mechanics** as a small illustration; possibly a **condensed table** if the build wants farmer-level realism. Our own drawing would be needed; do not reuse the figure.

### 17. ch. 23 outline, p. 1950-51: structure of the festival chapter
Concerns: 23:1-44. 
Shows: Milgrom's seven-part outline: introduction (vv. 1-2); sabbath (v. 3); Pesah and Unleavened Bread (vv. 4-8); firstfruits of barley and wheat (vv. 9-22, with a poor-law note, v. 22); Warning Blasts (vv. 23-25); Day of Purgation (vv. 26-32); Booths (vv. 33-43, with a four-species/booth appendix, vv. 39-43); compliance report (v. 44).
Facts: two halves, flagged by the same closing formula "I am YHWH your God" (vv. 22, 43): spring/first-month festivals plus their 50-day extension (vv. 5-22) and seventh-month festivals (vv. 23-36); an original subscript at vv. 37-38. The first and seventh months are matched, each with a 15th-of-the-month seven-day festival with a following extra day. Seven rest days counted: first and seventh days of Unleavened Bread, the Wheat festival, Warning Blasts, Purgation, first day of Booths, the day after Booths. The people (not the priests) are addressed; "present a food gift" (vv. 8, 25, 27, 36) shows the public cult is the people's duty.
Feeds: **calendar** (spine), **unit map** (section breaks).

### 24. Outline of ch. 25, pp. 2148-49 (text)
Facts: A sabbatical and jubilee (vv. 2-22): sabbatical (vv. 2-7: six years sow, seventh rest; the land's yield is for all, owner, slaves, hired hands, resident aliens, animals); jubilee (vv. 8-22: calendar v. 8, proclamation vv. 9-10, explication vv. 11-13); leasing of land and the jubilee (vv. 14-19); reassurance about food (vv. 20-22). B redemption of property (vv. 23-34): basic principle (23-24), redemption of a patrimony (25-28), sale of houses (29-31), Levitical property (32-34). C the indebted Israelite who lost land (35-38; no interest). D the debt-slave (39-55): Israelite treated as a hired worker, released at the jubilee (39-43); aside on owning foreign slaves (44-46); Israelite sold to a resident alien (47-54) must be redeemed.
Mechanics (our own table; verses are Milgrom's reading and the text itself):
| Situation | Who acts | Rule | Return/result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sale of ancestral field | seller or nearer kin (go'el, v. 25) | sale is a lease priced by the years left to the jubilee (vv. 15-16); deduct sabbaticals and disasters | land reverts at jubilee; if the seller prospers he may buy it back with the remaining value (vv. 26-27); the buyer's investment is repaid |
| House in a walled city | seller | redeem within one full year (vv. 29-30) | after a year the sale is permanent and does not revert at the jubilee |
| House in an unwalled village; Levitical cities | | treated as fields (v. 31); Levites' houses always redeemable (v. 32-33) | revert at the jubilee; pasture not sold (v. 34) |
| Israelite sold to a resident alien | the person, or an uncle, cousin or nearest kinsman (vv. 48-49) | price = years until the jubilee x hired-worker wages (vv. 50-52) | free at the jubilee with his children (v. 54) |
| Impoverished fellow Israelite | the community | support, no interest or profit on loans, no slave labor (vv. 35-43) | |
Milgrom: land and Israelites belong to God (vv. 23, 42, 55), so neither can be sold in perpetuity; persons and land are leased, not sold. He refuses to split the chapter into strata.

## Where Milgrom's reconstruction differs from another he discusses
- **The "sabbath" of 23:11, 15:** he reads it as the "sabbath-week", so the omer is waved on the first Sunday after harvest begins (pre-H layer), against the Festivalian (the first day of Unleavened Bread), Sabbatarian (the weekly sabbath, Qumran) and rabbinic readings; Table 1 shows the Qumran side.
- **Sabbatical timing (Tables 2-4):** he rejects Rashi (all-fall calendar) and Morgenstern (all-spring) and endorses Ramban's fall-calendar sabbatical and jubilee in two consecutive years; he also lists a minority view that vv. 20-22 once followed v. 7.
- **Lev 24:13-23:** Milgrom's long palistrophe with talion at the center versus Chavel's two parallel blocs (divine and human) in the same volume (Comment D). The two diagrams place the "alien equals citizen" clause differently.
- **Day of Purgation:** 23:27-32 is centered on the penalties, unlike 16:29-31, which is centered on purgation.
- **Authorship:** he says Lev 23 depends on Num 28-29 (P earlier, H later), against critics who date H earlier; his layer labels (Pre-H1, Pre-H2, H, Hr) go beyond most readings.
- **Jubilee in history:** the Major Dates table argues, with 50-year gaps, that the jubilee was observed (Hezekiah, Zedekiah, Cyrus, Nehemiah); the evidence is suggestive, not agreed.
