## Tier reasoning

One line per unit. Full units and peaks are the plan's; the rest is judgement, checked against the commentaries.

- 01: full. The model offering: the pattern (bring near, lay hand, slaughter, blood, burn) the other offering units refer back to.
- 02: condensed. Short, with little narrative; its key verses (2:1-3, 2:13) carry the grain offering and the salt of the covenant.
- 03: condensed. Close variant of 01; difference is what is offered and the fat and blood prohibition (3:16-17).
- 04: condensed. Four graded cases (priest, congregation, ruler, individual), then the scaled offering of 5:1-13, all one speech (4:1 to 5:13; next formula 5:14). Milgrom reads 5:1-13 as a distinct category (vol. 1 pp. 307-10); show that inside the unit, not as a split.
- 05: condensed. Sacred things and neighbour wrong; the key verses are 5:14-16 and 6:1-7 (the fifth added). Heb numbering shifts at 6:1.
- 06: digest. Rubrics for priests and portions (Milgrom's "administrative order", vol. 1 p. 378); one subscript-closed block (7:37-38). A summary serves.
- 07: full. Narrative of the ordination, the Exodus 29 pattern; the obedience formula "as Yahweh commanded" returns.
- 08: full. The first sacrifices, the glory and fire (9:23-24); the pair with 10.
- 09: full, peak. The fire that consumes Nadab and Abihu, and its place after 9:24; the book's turning point. Milgrom's ring pairs it with 24:10-23 (vol. 2 p. 1365).
- 10: condensed. A long list; the key verses are the principle (11:44-47). Tables show the animal lists.
- 11: digest. A short case; brings the ḥaṭṭaʾt and ʿolah for the woman (12:6-8).
- 12: condensed. Cases come in a pattern with a verdict formula; tables and strips suit it. Key verses: 13:45-46 and 13:59.
- 13: digest. Cleansing rite in two stages and the house case; the rite is a sequence, so a summary plus a later `ritual` strip.
- 14: digest. The cases are parallel; the closing statement (15:31) is the one verse needed.
- 15: full, peak. The two goats, the confession and the sanctuary purged; the book's centre (Morales pp. 38-39). Milgrom's outline of the rite, vol. 1 pp. 1059-60.
- 16: condensed. Opens Part Two: five laws with no eating of blood at the axis (17:10-12; Milgrom vol. 2 pp. 1448-49). The pivot is 17:11, which Morales reads as the key to 1-16 (p. 39).
- 17: condensed. Tight list; a table of unions and the frame (18:1-5, 24-30).
- 18: full, peak. Holiness for the whole people: the love command (19:18), neighbour, resident alien, mixed cases. Centre of Milgrom's ring, flanked by 18 and 20 (vol. 2 p. 1365).
- 19: condensed. Matches the wrongs of 18 to penalties; the karet and stoning cases are tabular.
- 20: condensed. The holiness of the high priest and the blemished; key verses are 21:6, 21:10-15.
- 21: digest. Who may eat holy gifts and what is acceptable; ends with the speech-closing refrain (22:31-33), and 22:29-30 echoes 19:5-6 to close 19-22 (Milgrom vol. 2 pp. 1323-24).
- 22: condensed. A calendar; ends 23:44. Key verses are the Sabbath (23:3), the Day of Atonement (23:27-32) and Booths. Sections follow Milgrom's outline (vol. 3 pp. 1950-51).
- 23: full, 24:1-9 condensed. One unit: Morales reads the story as extending the lamp and bread, with 24:1-9 the heart of 23-25 (pp. 224, 236). Milgrom outlines the halves apart (vol. 3 pp. 2084, 2101-02).
- 24: full, peak. Sabbath year and Jubilee (25:1-7, 8-22). Ends at 25:22, Milgrom's main division (vol. 3 pp. 2148-49); the Masoretic setumah is at 25:24.
- 25: full, peak. The principle (25:23-24), then redemption of land, house and person, with the goʾel; the Exodus echo in 25:42, 55. Split from 24 for length; Milgrom keeps the chapter one composition.
- 26: full, peak. Opens with the bridge 26:1-2, which binds 25 and 26 (Milgrom vol. 3 pp. 2274-76, 2284-85; the Masoretic paragraph runs 25:47-26:2). Then the blessings, five rounds of curses and the remembered covenant; the pair with Deuteronomy 28.
- 27: digest. Appendix on vows and valuations, closed by the subscript (27:34).

## Digests

One line per unit for the peaks path (`card-condensed.md`: two to four sentences, 80 words at most). Offering and purity names follow the working titles and change with Lane's term calls.

- 01: Yahweh calls Moshe from the Tent and gives the first offering. A worshipper brings an unblemished male from the herd, the flock or the birds, lays a hand on it and slaughters it; the priests dash its blood on the altar and burn it whole. It rises as a soothing aroma to Yahweh, the pattern the later offerings follow.
- 02: The grain offering is fine flour with oil and frankincense, raw or baked. The priest burns a token handful on the altar, and the rest belongs to Aharon and his sons as most holy. No leaven or honey goes on the altar, and every offering is salted with the salt of the covenant.
- 03: The peace offering comes from the herd or the flock, male or female. As with the burnt offering, the offerer lays a hand on it and the blood goes on the altar, but only the fat around the inner organs is burned. The chapter ends with a lasting rule: Israel eats no fat and no blood.
- 04: When someone sins without meaning to, a purification offering is due, graded by who sinned: the anointed priest, the whole community, a leader, or an ordinary person. Its blood goes further in the more serious cases, as far as the veil. Then come cases of withheld testimony, unnoticed impurity and rash oaths, where the offering is scaled to what the person can afford.
- 05: Someone who misuses what belongs to Yahweh's holy things, even unknowingly, brings a ram and repays the loss plus a fifth. The same holds for wronging a neighbour by deceit, theft or a false oath: restore what was taken, add a fifth, and bring the ram. Wrong against a neighbour is also unfaithfulness to Yahweh.
- 06: Instructions to the priests for each offering already described: the fire on the altar never goes out, and each offering has its own handling. Most of the rules settle which portions the priests eat, where, and who may eat the peace offering. A closing line says these were commanded at Sinai.
- 07: Moshe does what Yahweh commanded in Exodus 29. Before the whole community he washes Aharon and his sons, dresses them, anoints the Tent and Aharon, and offers the ordination sacrifices, putting blood on their right ear, thumb and big toe. They stay at the Tent's entrance for seven days.
- 08: On the eighth day Aharon offers for himself and for the people for the first time. When he and Moshe come out and bless the people, Yahweh's glory appears to all, and fire comes out from Yahweh and consumes the offering. The people shout and fall on their faces.
- 09: Nadab and Abihu offer fire Yahweh had not commanded, and fire comes out from Yahweh and consumes them. Aharon is silent, the priests may not mourn in the usual way, and Aharon is told to separate holy from common and clean from unclean. The chapter ends with a dispute over an uneaten purification offering, which Moshe accepts.
- 10: Which animals Israel may eat: land animals that chew the cud and have split hooves, water creatures with fins and scales, certain birds and winged insects. Touching their carcasses makes a person unclean until evening. The reason given is holiness: "be holy, because I am holy."
- 11: After childbirth a mother is unclean for seven days for a boy, fourteen for a girl, and waits longer before she may touch holy things. At the end she brings a burnt offering and a purification offering, and the priest makes atonement for her. A poor mother may bring two birds.
- 12: A sore, swelling or patch on the skin is shown to the priest, who examines it, sometimes isolates the person for seven days, and declares it clean or unclean. Someone declared unclean tears their clothes, cries "Unclean!" and lives outside the camp. Mould in cloth or leather is judged the same way.
- 13: The person healed of tsaraʿat is restored in stages: two birds and running water outside the camp, washing and shaving, then offerings on the eighth day with blood and oil on ear, thumb and toe. A poorer person brings less. A house with spreading mould is inspected, repaired or torn down, and then cleansed.
- 14: Discharges from the body make a person unclean, along with whatever they lie or sit on: an abnormal discharge, an emission of semen, menstruation, prolonged bleeding. Washing and waiting end most of these, and the abnormal ones need offerings on the eighth day. The aim is stated at the end: Israel's uncleanness must not defile Yahweh's dwelling among them.
- 15: After his sons' deaths, Aharon is told how and when he may enter behind the veil. Once a year he purifies the inner sanctuary, the Tent and the altar with the blood of a bull and a goat, then confesses Israel's sins over a second goat and sends it into the wilderness. Israel rests and humbles itself, and is made clean before Yahweh.
- 16: Any ox, sheep or goat that is slaughtered must be brought to the Tent as an offering, not killed elsewhere or offered to goat-demons. No one, Israelite or resident foreigner, may eat blood, because the life is in the blood and Yahweh gave it on the altar to make atonement. The blood of game is poured out and covered with earth.
- 17: Israel must not live as Egypt and Canaan do but by Yahweh's rules, which give life. A list follows of forbidden unions with close relatives, along with other forbidden practices, including giving children to Molek. The land vomited out the nations who did these things and will do the same to Israel.
- 18: "Be holy, because I, Yahweh your God, am holy." Holiness covers parents, Sabbath, idols and offerings, but also leaving the edges of the harvest, paying workers on time, fair courts, honest weights, the deaf and the blind, the elderly and the foreigner. At the centre: love your neighbour as yourself, and love the foreigner too.
- 19: The wrongs of chapter 18 return with their penalties: giving a child to Molek, consulting mediums, cursing parents, and the forbidden unions. Some are punished by death, others by being cut off or dying childless. Yahweh calls Israel to be holy and separate, as he has separated them from the nations.
- 20: Priests may mourn only their closest kin, must keep their marriages within set limits, and must not profane themselves. The high priest is held to more: no mourning even for parents, and marriage only to a virgin of his people. A descendant of Aharon with a physical defect may eat the holy food but may not approach the altar.
- 21: Priests must not touch holy food while unclean, and only their households may eat it. Offerings must be without defect, with specific defects listed. Yahweh's holy name must not be profaned: he makes Israel holy and brought them out of Egypt.
- 22: Yahweh's appointed times: the weekly Sabbath, Passover and Unleavened Bread, the first sheaf, the Feast of Weeks fifty days later, then in the seventh month the trumpet day, the Day of Atonement and the Feast of Booths. Each has its offerings and days of rest, and Booths recalls the wilderness shelters.
- 23: Pure oil keeps the lamp burning before Yahweh, and twelve loaves are set out each Sabbath. Then a man with an Israelite mother and an Egyptian father curses the Name in a fight, and Yahweh gives the verdict: the community stones him outside the camp. Life for life, injury for injury, and one law for the foreigner and the native.
- 24: In the land, every seventh year the land itself rests: no sowing or pruning, and the yield is for everyone to eat. After seven of those cycles, the fiftieth year is the Jubilee, proclaimed with a horn on the Day of Atonement, when each person returns to their family land. Yahweh promises enough to eat in the years of rest.
- 25: The land is Yahweh's, so it cannot be sold for good; Israel lives on it as foreigners and tenants. When someone grows poor and sells land or even themselves, the nearest relative should redeem them, and the Jubilee restores them anyway. Israelites are Yahweh's servants, brought out of Egypt, so they must not be held as slaves.
- 26: Keep no idols and keep Yahweh's Sabbaths. If Israel walks in his laws, there will be rain, harvest, peace, and Yahweh will walk among them. If they refuse, five rounds of punishment grow worse, ending in exile while the land enjoys its Sabbaths. Yet if they confess, Yahweh will remember his covenant with their ancestors.
- 27: People, animals, houses and fields can be dedicated to Yahweh by a vow, each with a set value in silver, and most can be redeemed by paying the value plus a fifth. Firstborn animals, things devoted to destruction, and the tithe already belong to Yahweh. A closing line says these were commanded at Sinai.
